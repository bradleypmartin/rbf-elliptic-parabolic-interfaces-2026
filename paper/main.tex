\begin{abstract}
A diffusion coefficient can vary smoothly on the scale of the grid, jump
across an interface, or change smoothly over a width $\delta$ smaller than
the node spacing $h$. Through such a sub-grid edge, standard high-order
stencils that sample the coefficient at the nodes are first order until the
grid resolves the edge, with a knee at $h \approx \delta$, and the
interface-aware stencils built for a jump, applied at the edge's centre, sit
on an error floor of order $\delta$ and leave it once the grid resolves the
edge. We keep the nodes and replace the polynomial basis of the stencils
that see the edge by \emph{seeds}. The seeds are the profiles of solutions
polynomial in time, continued through the edge by ordinary differential
equations along the normal or along the edge's own coordinates; in one
dimension they are the classical formal powers of the Sturm--Liouville
operator, and as $\delta \to 0$ they reduce to the interface-aware stencils
of a jump. On scattered nodes the resulting RBF-FD stencils are fourth order
at every $\delta$ tested, elliptic and parabolic, with no switch between
regimes. We compare them with the coefficient treatments of diffusion
practice. The comparison is against coefficient sampling on the same nodes
and against our implementation of the cell harmonic and arithmetic means, a
widened edge and, in one dimension, the conservative scheme with exact face
conductances, on one contrast and one test problem per dimension. Through an
edge the grid does not resolve none of them is better than second order; the
conservative scheme is exact at the one-dimensional equilibrium and, on the
sinusoidal medium at $\delta = 0$, ahead of the seeds below 801 nodes, where
the seeds' line is pre-asymptotic; on the scattered nodes none improves on
sampling by more than a factor of 1.75 in the RMS norm, and the seeds lie
2.3 to 4.7 orders below the best of them over 1250 to 40,000 nodes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% TRACE: stiff 1.1 (the setting, the three regimes), 1.2 (the chain), 1.7
% and 2.2 (the construction on a smooth edge), 2.5 (what the 1-D study
% states), 5.3 (what the 2-D study states); plan sections 1 and 3;
% LITERATURE.md 6a for the prior-work paragraph and 6b for the claim
% sentences (#43). Drafted by #45. Numbers are traced paragraph by
% paragraph below; every cache-backed one is a check in

Heat conduction through a composite material is governed by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:heat}
  u_t = \nabla \cdot (\alpha \nabla u) \equiv L u
\end{equation}
and, at equilibrium, by $L u = 0$, with a conductivity $\alpha > 0$ that
changes sharply between materials. At the two ends of the range of media
the numerical picture is settled. Where $\alpha$ varies smoothly on
the scale of the grid, a high-order stencil samples it at the nodes and
keeps its order. Where $\alpha$ jumps across an interface, the temperature
and the flux $\alpha\, \partial_n u$ are continuous while the gradient is
not, a standard stencil across the interface is first order, and the
remedies rebuild the stencils or the elements that the interface cuts so
that they satisfy the interface conditions (Section~\ref{sec:priorwork}).
One of them, the interface-aware RBF-FD stencils of
\cite{Martin2016,MartinFornberg2017heat} that this paper extends, keeps
fourth order on scattered nodes, elliptic and parabolic, with a few rows of
nodes placed across each interface. Between the two ends lies a third case:
an edge that is smooth, so that no interface condition applies, but
changes over a width $\delta$ smaller than the node spacing $h$. Every
material edge is smooth on some scale, a model that resolves an edge on a
fine grid presents it as sub-grid on a coarser one, and any regularisation
of a jump produces one.

% TRACE: the 1-D knee, stiff 2.5 (1) (the two-constant medium's ramp
% problem at delta = 0.01 and 0.0025, h = 2 delta and h = delta/2; the drop
% "100 to 200 times", checked as bounds); the 2-D knee, stiff 5.3 (2)
% (0.86-0.94 of the jump's error while h >~ 6 delta, 0.081-0.084 at
% h ~ delta, the bulk stencils' order 5 below the knee). "Falls by four" is
% the first-order line over a factor 4 in h, not a measurement.
A standard stencil through a sub-grid edge samples $\alpha$ on its two
sides, sees a jump, and treats the coefficient as smooth between its nodes,
which is the first-order failure of the jump case. It recovers once the grid
resolves the edge. In the one-dimensional ramp problem of
Section~\ref{sec:knee1d}, the error of the standard fourth-order operator
through a $9 : 1$ edge of width $\delta = 0.01$ or $0.0025$ falls by 100 to
200 times between $h = 2\delta$ and $h = \delta/2$, where a first-order line
falls by four. On scattered nodes in two dimensions
(Section~\ref{sec:naive2d}) its elliptic error stays at $0.86$ to $0.94$ of
its error at the jump while $h \gtrsim 6\delta$, falls to $0.081$ to
$0.084$ of it at $h \approx \delta$ for the three widths that reach it, and
converges at the fifth order of the bulk stencils below. We call the turn at
$h \approx \delta$ the knee.

% TRACE: c = (a - b) ln(a/b)/(2ab), stiff 1.7 and 2.2 (E3.3's closed form,
% heat1d.exact.edge_resistance_deficit); 0.11 at 1600 nodes and
% delta = 0.01, stiff 2.5 (1) (the two-constant medium's equilibrium);
% "only while h >~ 3 delta, by at most a factor of 6", stiff 5.3 (3).
The interface-aware stencils do not reach this regime either. Applied with
the centre of the edge as the interface and the two sides' coefficients as
if $\delta$ were $0$, they converge to the jump problem's solution, which is
not the smooth problem's: the resistance $\int dx / \alpha$ of a $\tanh$ edge
between conductivities $a$ and $b$ falls short of the jump's by $c\,\delta$,
$c = (a - b) \ln(a/b) / (2ab)$, so their error sits on a floor of order
$\delta$ while the grid does not resolve the edge. Once it does, the rebuilt
rows keep enforcing a kink that the solution lacks, and their error grows:
through a $9 : 1$ edge with $\delta = 0.01$ it reaches $0.11$ at 1600
nodes in the one-dimensional equilibrium of
Section~\ref{sec:construction1d}. On scattered nodes they
beat the standard stencils only while $h \gtrsim 3\delta$, and by at most a
factor of $6$. Using them means knowing $\delta$ and switching them off.

% TRACE: the chain, stiff 1.2 (eq. 1.2's definition; the heat polynomials,
% the even and odd seeds as the continuity conditions); 1.3 (the
% first-order form); 1.4 (the jump as the limit); the companion,
% LITERATURE.md 1b O1's last obligation (stiff 1.2: its u-field chain with
% rho = 1, K = alpha).
This paper keeps everything about the scheme except the basis of the
stencils that see the edge. In place of the monomials a stencil is made
exact on \emph{seeds}, the $t = 0$ profiles of solutions of \eqref{eq:heat}
that are polynomial in time. With a constant $\alpha$ these profiles are
the polynomials, the initial values of the heat polynomials
\cite{RosenbloomWidder1959}. In one dimension, with $L = \px \alpha \px$
and anchored at a stencil's evaluation point $x_e$, they form the chain
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:chain}
  \phi_0 = 1, \qquad
  \alpha \phi_1' = \alpha(x_e), \qquad
  L \phi_k = k (k - 1)\, \alpha(x_e)\, \phi_{k-2} \quad (k \ge 2),
\end{equation}
with $\phi_k(x_e) = 0$ for $k \ge 1$ and $\phi_k'(x_e) = 0$ for $k \ge 2$,
integrated through the edge as a first-order system of ordinary
differential equations that never differentiates $\alpha$
(Section~\ref{sec:chain}). The seed $\phi_1$ has a constant flux, and
$\phi_2$ is the profile of a solution whose rate of change $u_t$ is
constant; in general the even seeds carry
$\partial_t^k u = \text{const}$ and the odd ones
$\alpha\, \px \partial_t^k u = \text{const}$, which are the interface
conditions of a jump. Read across a jump, the chain is the translated
polynomial basis of the interface-aware stencils
(Section~\ref{sec:jumplimit}), so one construction covers the jump, the
sub-grid edge and the resolved edge. In one dimension it is classical
(Section~\ref{sec:priorwork}). It is also the construction of our companion
paper for the wave equation \cite{Martin2026seeds}, specialised to a single
field: the two interface conditions of diffusion, on $u$ and on its flux,
are both conditions on $u$, so one chain serves every stencil.

% TRACE: stiff 3.2-3.4 (the straight feature: seeds polynomial in x' with
% coefficient functions marched in y'), 3.5 and 4.6 (a frozen normal profile
% O(1)-inconsistent where alpha varies along an unresolved edge; route (a)),
% 3.10 (the tangential chain in the curve's own coordinates), 3.11 (the
% ring's exact width, series and flux seeds); the flux-seed sentence is
% LITERATURE.md 6b's, required wherever the flux seeds are named (K9).
In two dimensions the seeds replace the polynomial augmentation of the
interface-aware RBF-FD stencils, and the Gaussian part and the
saddle-point solve stay as they were. Where $\alpha$ varies along the
normal only, as across a straight edge, seeds polynomial in the tangential
coordinate turn the chain into a triangular system of ordinary differential
equations in the normal coordinate, one short march per stencil
(Section~\ref{sec:straight}). Where the edge is curved or $\alpha$ varies
along it, a profile frozen along the normal is inconsistent at $O(1)$
through an unresolved edge, and the chain is marched in the curve's own
coordinates instead (Section~\ref{sec:curved}). A resistive layer thinner
than the stencil, the insulating ring of \cite{MartinFornberg2017heat},
needs its exact width, its own series and seeds of one degree higher that
carry the flux (Section~\ref{sec:ring}). An imperfect contact costs
strong-form schemes a derivative in the jump conditions, as Lombard and
Piraux observed for elastic waves \cite{LombardPiraux2003,LombardPiraux2006};
the flux seeds add the functions of one degree higher that carry the flux,
and nothing else.

% TRACE: the comparators, stiff 1.1 (baselines), 2.4 and 4.9 (the
% treatments), plan 3.4; the 1-D elliptic degeneracy on constant pieces,
% stiff 1.8 and 2.5 (4); the 2-D elliptic problem as a real test, stiff
% 5.3 (5).
We measure the seeds in one and two dimensions, on elliptic and parabolic
problems, against three baselines: the standard stencils with $\alpha$
sampled at the nodes; the interface-aware stencils of the jump applied at
the edge's centre; and the coefficient treatments of diffusion practice,
which change the coefficient and keep the scheme: harmonic and arithmetic
means of $\alpha$ over a cell (over a disc on scattered nodes), the edge
widened to the grid, and, in one dimension, the conservative scheme with
exact face conductances. On constant pieces the one-dimensional equilibrium
cannot rank these methods, since its solution has a constant flux and lies
in the span of the first two seeds, on which the seeds and the exact face
conductances are both exact (Section~\ref{sec:elliptic1d}); the
one-dimensional ranking is the parabolic problem's. In two dimensions the
tangential variation of the solution takes it out of that span, and the
equilibrium is a real test.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}
\label{sec:priorwork}
% Placed from LITERATURE.md 6a (#43), cut as 6a allows, in its order: (1) the
% HODIE and fitted-operator sentence is dropped (Section 3 cites them);
% (2) the jump methods are cut to the IIM and CutFEM keys (K8: LeVeque-Li,
% Li-Ito; Hansbo-Hansbo, Burman et al.) and the two post-2016 ones; (3) the
% resistive-layer sentence moves to Section 5.4 (sec:ring, where it waits
% as a comment for #48), leaving one clause here. The first sentence, the
% FLAME and LOD clauses and the last four sentences are verbatim: they carry
% the obligations of LITERATURE.md 1a K2-K5, K10 and 1b O1, and it stays one
% paragraph because O1 asks for K3-K6 in the claim's paragraph. About 480
% words: 6a's 250-350 target is retired (its note, Brad, 2026-09-24). Any
% change to the novelty language re-opens the ledger first.
% TRACE: 1.75, stiff 5.3 (10), paper/data/heat2d_stiff_treatments*.json
% (paper_numbers.py, where "abstract, §1; stiff §5.3 (10)").
In one dimension the construction is classical in every part, and we claim
nothing for it. The first seed, the constant-flux function
$\alpha_e \int d\xi/\alpha$, is over one cell the exact face conductance of
Tikhonov and Samarskii's homogeneous schemes
\cite{TikhonovSamarskii1962,Samarskii2001} and, for a piecewise-constant
coefficient, the harmonic-mean interface conductivity of finite-volume heat
conduction \cite{Patankar1980}; the conservative scheme built on it, exact
at the source-free equilibrium, is second order \cite{PanXuLi2026}. The
whole chain is the representation of Sturm--Liouville solutions by
recursive integrals, as power series in the spectral parameter
\cite{Kravchenko2008,KravchenkoPorter2010}, taken from the anchor; for a
constant coefficient its time-polynomial solutions are the heat polynomials
\cite{RosenbloomWidder1959,Widder1975}. Three-point schemes of any order
for $(k u')'$ with a piecewise-smooth $k$, whose coefficients come from
Cauchy problems integrated across each cell, are the exact and truncated
difference schemes \cite{SamarskiiMakarov1990,GavrilyukEtAl2011};
three-point schemes of any order exist across a jump too
\cite{Gartland1989}, one of them built by carrying Taylor data across the
step \cite{Sujecki2010}. In two dimensions, local solutions of the equation that satisfy the
interface conditions are the basis of the Trefftz difference schemes of
FLAME, on irregular stencils too \cite{Tsukerman2006,Tsukerman2010}, and of
the tailored finite point methods \cite{Huang2009,HanZhang2012}; Galerkin
methods build their bases from local solves
\cite{BabuskaCalozOsborn1994,HouWu1997,ChuGrahamHou2010,OwhadiZhang2007,MalqvistPeterseim2014}
and reach high order through coefficients the mesh does not resolve
\cite{Maier2021,HauckPeterseim2023}, and the quasi-Trefftz spaces, one of
them for the heat equation, build polynomial local solutions by Taylor
expansion \cite{ImbertGerardMoiolaPerinatiStocker2025,GomezPerinatiStocker2026},
which cannot reach through an edge thinner than the stencil
\cite{Martin2026seeds}. For a jump, the interface-aware RBF-FD stencils
this work generalises \cite{Martin2016,MartinFornberg2017heat} stand beside
the immersed interface method and unfitted finite elements
\cite{LeVequeLi1994,LiIto2006,HansboHansbo2002,BurmanEtAl2015}, now of
fourth and sixth order \cite{FengZhao2020,FengHanMinev2024}, all of which
take the jump as given. A resistive layer thinner than the grid is an
imperfect interface in the limit
\cite{SanchezPalencia1974,PerrusselPoignard2013,Hashin2001}, which
Section~\ref{sec:ring} takes up. The standing alternative changes the
coefficient rather than the stencil: harmonic or arithmetic means over a
cell \cite{Patankar1980,EwingIlievLazarov2001,GoodeAppel1992}, averaging for
layering finer than the grid \cite{MoskowEtAl1999,DavydychevaDruskinHabashy2003},
or a regularised coefficient \cite{TornbergEngquist2003,TornbergEngquist2006}.
Those methods take the cell or the width from the grid; here the width is a
property of the medium, below the node spacing, and the aim is the scheme's
full order through it. We found no prior instance of stencils on an
unchanged scattered node set whose basis is continued through a sub-grid
smooth edge by ordinary differential equations, nor of such stencils
reducing to a jump construction as $\delta \to 0$; the search is logged in
the repository's \texttt{LITERATURE.md}. We ran the cell means, a widened
edge and, in one dimension, the conservative scheme with exact face
conductances, as our implementation of each on one problem per dimension:
through an edge the grid does not resolve none is better than second order,
and on the scattered nodes none improves on sampling the coefficient by
more than a factor of $1.75$ in the root-mean-square error. We claim nothing
beyond that measurement.

\subsection{The problem and its interface conditions}
\label{sec:problem}
% TRACE: stiff 1.1 (eq. 51-55 of the dissertation, EABE eq. 1: continuity
% of D^k u and alpha d_x D^k u; the tanh edge; the three regimes), 3.1
% (the 2-D edge in the signed normal distance, composed edge by edge);
% 2.1 (the tails: within two ulps of the pieces at 19 delta, the pieces
% to the bit beyond 20 delta); c = (a - b) ln(a/b)/(2ab), stiff 1.7 and
% 2.2; 8.79 for the 9 : 1 edge, stiff 2.5 (1), paper/data/heat1d_stiff.json
% (paper_numbers.py, where "§2.1; stiff §2.5 (1)"); the norms and fits,
% stiff 5.3's preamble and 5.5, port notes 2.10.
On a domain $\Omega$ in one or two dimensions we solve \eqref{eq:heat} with
Dirichlet data on $\partial \Omega$, and its equilibrium $L u = 0$, the
elliptic problem. The conductivity $\alpha > 0$ is one smooth function on
each side of every interface $\Gamma$. Across a jump the temperature and the
heat flux are continuous at all times, hence so are all their time
derivatives, and $\partial_t^k u = L^k u$ turns these into conditions on
spatial derivatives,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:continuity}
  [L^k u] = 0, \qquad [\alpha\, \partial_n L^k u] = 0 \qquad \text{on }
  \Gamma, \quad k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots,
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]$ is the difference of the two sides' limits, $n$ the normal
to $\Gamma$, and each side's $L$ is taken with that side's $\alpha$
\cite{Martin2016,MartinFornberg2017heat}.

\para{A smooth edge}
With an edge width $\delta > 0$ the jump at an interface is replaced by a
$\tanh$ transition between the two pieces,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:edge}
  \alpha = \bigl(1 - \sigma(d / \delta)\bigr)\, \alpha^- + \sigma(d / \delta)\,
  \alpha^+, \qquad \sigma(z) = \tfrac12 (1 + \tanh z),
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the signed distance to the interface, $x - x_c$ in one
dimension and the distance along the normal through the foot point on the
curve in two, positive on the side of $\alpha^+$. The pieces keep their own
variation, and $\delta = 0$ is the jump. A band between two interfaces is
blended twice, from the outside piece into the inside one across the lower
interface and back across the upper, so that a band wider than its edges
has two independent transitions; Section~\ref{sec:ring} defines the smooth
form of a layer thinner than its edges. Beyond $19\delta$ from its centre
an edge is its pieces to within two units in the last place.

\para{Three regimes}
For $\delta \gg h$ a stencil sees a smooth coefficient. For $\delta = 0$ it
sees a jump, and \eqref{eq:continuity} applies. For $0 < \delta \lesssim h$
it sees what looks to it like a jump between two of its nodes, but no
interface condition applies, since $\alpha$ is smooth, and the smooth
problem and the jump problem have different solutions. They differ at
first order in $\delta$: in one dimension the resistance $\int dx / \alpha$
of a $\tanh$ edge between constant pieces $a$ and $b$ falls short of the
jump's by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:deficit}
  c\, \delta, \qquad c = \frac{(a - b) \ln (a / b)}{2 a b},
\end{equation}
which is $8.79\,\delta$ for the $9 : 1$ edge of Section~\ref{sec:setting1d}
and is positive for either ordering of $a$ and $b$.

\para{Norms and orders}
In one dimension an error is the relative $\ell^2$ error at the nodes,
$\|e\|_2 / \|u\|_2$, and a rate is $\log_2$ of the ratio of consecutive
errors as the node count doubles. In two dimensions an error is the
root-mean-square (RMS) error over all nodes, the Dirichlet nodes included,
and an order is the least-squares slope of $\log e$ against $\log h$ over
the node counts named. A maximum-norm error is named as such wherever one
is quoted, and every caption says which norm it shows.

The one-dimensional problem is \eqref{eq:heat} with $L = \px \alpha \px$ on
$[-1, 1]$ with Dirichlet ends. A grid has $n$ equispaced nodes with both
ends on nodes, so $h = 2 / (n - 1)$. Two media are used, each at
$\delta = 0$, $0.04$, $0.01$ and $0.0025$:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \emph{two-constant medium}, $\alpha = 1/9$ for $x < 0$ and $1$ for
  $x > 0$, the single interface of the time-dependent test in the code
  behind \cite{Martin2016}, which the dissertation does not report; the
  edge lies midway between two nodes when $n$ is even;
\item the \emph{sinusoidal medium} of \cite[eq.~(75)]{Martin2016},
  $\alpha = 0.1 + 0.4 \sin 2\pi x$ on the layer $[0, \tfrac12]$ and $1$
  elsewhere, whose two edges both have the contrast $10 : 1$, since the
  sinusoid vanishes at both; both edges fall on nodes when $n - 1$ is a
  multiple of $4$.
\end{itemize}
Each medium carries two problems: the equilibrium with $u(-1) = 1$ and
$u(1) = 0$, and the \emph{ramp problem}, which starts from $u = 0$, holds
$u(1, t) = 0$, raises $u(-1, t)$ from $0$ to $1$ over $0 \le t \le 1$ by
the $C^\infty$ step
$e^{-1/t} / (e^{-1/t} + e^{-1/(1-t)})$, and is read at $t = 2$. (The
original code ramps with a logistic function, whose value and derivatives at
$t = 0$ are small but not zero; the mismatch with the zero start at the
corner would cap a fourth-order study.) Section~\ref{sec:media1d} gives the
reference solutions.

The \emph{naive} operator is $D_x A D_x$ \cite[eq.~(76)]{Martin2016}, with
$D_x$ the fourth-order first-derivative matrix of Fornberg's weights
\cite{Fornberg1988}, one-sided at the ends, and $A = \operatorname{diag}
\alpha(x_j)$, which gives nine-point rows in the interior. It is the form in
which a standard stencil sees a jump at all: a direct discretisation of
$\alpha u_{xx} + \alpha' u_x$ with the coefficients sampled at the nodes is
blind to the jumps, and its error on the sinusoidal medium stays at $0.22$
as the grid is refined. Time stepping is the fourth-order backward
differentiation formula (BD4) with $\Delta t = h$, one sparse LU
factorisation per operator and the Dirichlet values imposed at every step;
its three starting values come from classical Runge--Kutta sub-steps at half
the stability limit.

\para{Node sets}
A set of $n$ nodes is built as in \cite{MartinFornberg2017heat}. Rows hold
$m = \operatorname{round}(0.95 \sqrt n)$ nodes per unit length, so their
spacing is $h = 1/m$. Six fixed rows straddle each interface, three on each
side at normal distances $(\tfrac12 + j \tfrac{\sqrt 3}{2}) h$,
$j = 0, 1, 2$, the middle row on each side staggered by half a spacing
along the curve, so that the rows cross the interface in a hexagonal
pattern; a fixed row
lies on each Dirichlet curve; and the remaining nodes are placed by 100
steps of a simulated repulsion from a random start, kept clear of the
straddling rows. The ring is thinner than $h$ at every node count used, so
one set of six rows straddles its midline, and both of its interfaces pass
between the innermost pair. Unless a spread over node sets is quoted,
each node count has one node set, from a fixed random seed.

\para{RBF-FD}
RBF-FD \cite{FornbergFlyer2015primer,FornbergFlyer2015acta} builds each row
of a differentiation matrix from a stencil. For a stencil of nodes
$x_1, \ldots, x_{N_s}$ about an evaluation node $x_e$, the weights $w$ of a
linear operator $\mathcal{L}$ solve
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rbffd}
  \begin{bmatrix} \Phi & P \\ P^{\mathsf T} & 0 \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} w \\ w_* \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathcal{L}\, \varphi(\|x - x_i\|) \big|_{x_e} \\
    \mathcal{L}\, p_j(x) \big|_{x_e}
  \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
with $\Phi_{il} = \varphi(\|x_i - x_l\|)$ for the Gaussian
$\varphi(r) = e^{-(\varepsilon r)^2}$, $P_{ij} = p_j(x_i)$ over the
bivariate monomials $p_j$ up to a degree $p$ in coordinates scaled by the
stencil's radius, and $w_*$ discarded. The constraint rows make the weights
exact on the polynomials, which take over from the Gaussians under
refinement \cite{FlyerFornbergBayonaBarnett2016,BayonaFlyerFornbergBarnett2017};
the shape parameter is $\varepsilon = 0.4 / d$, with $d$ the distance from
$x_e$ to its nearest neighbour. Stencils have $42$ nodes and degree $5$ in
the bulk, and $30$ nodes and degree $4$ within $3 / \sqrt n$ of a Dirichlet
curve and wherever a $42$-node stencil would reach across an interface.
Of the latter, the interface-aware construction rebuilds the rows whose
$30$ nodes cross an interface (Section~\ref{sec:jump}); the seeds rebuild
the rows whose stencils see an edge (Section~\ref{sec:assembly2d}).

\para{Operators and solvers}
The naive operator is $D_x A D_x + D_y A D_y$, the two-dimensional form of
\cite[eq.~(76)]{Martin2016}, with every node's rows of $D_x$ and $D_y$ built
on its own stencil, so that it reaches the neighbours of the neighbours.
The direct operator applies $\alpha \nabla^2 u + \nabla \alpha \cdot \nabla
u$ at each node through one stencil; it is blind to a jump and serves as
the control where the grid resolves the edge. Dirichlet rows are the
identity. The elliptic systems are solved directly with SuperLU; the
iterative study applies GMRES and BiCGSTAB to the interior system, with and
without an incomplete LU factorisation and the diagonal-dominance
preconditioner of \cite[App.~B]{Martin2016}. The parabolic problem of
case 1 is marched by BD4 with $\Delta t = h$ from the exact solution's
history, $3$ to $19$ steps to $t = 0.1$.

\subsection{Interface-aware stencils for a jump}
\label{sec:jump}
% TRACE: port notes 1.1 (the 1-D translated basis: degree 4, EABE eq. 24,
% three or four rebuilt rows per interface; two interfaces translate
% twice, 1.6), 2.3 (the 2-D continuity rows: the first p + 1 - 2k
% coefficients of D^k u and p - 2k of the flux, 15 for p = 4; the
% curvature entering twice, EABE eq. 29-30 / dissertation eq. 82-83; the
% frame at the nearest point, in stencil-radius units), 2.4 (the warp:
% slope ratio alpha^- / alpha^+, value and alpha d_n continuous, first
% order in the RBF part; EABE 2.2.4); stiff 1.2 (the even and odd seeds as
% the five conditions), 1.4 and 5.3 (4) (the jump limit); stiff 1.1 and 3.1
% (the delta = 0 construction as named).
The seeds extend the interface-aware stencils of
\cite{Martin2016,MartinFornberg2017heat}, whose wave-equation form is in
\cite{MartinFornbergStCyr2015,MartinFornberg2017}. We summarise them in
the form the later sections use; the derivations are in those references.

\para{One dimension}
Expand $u$ on each side of an interface $x_c$ in a polynomial of degree
four about $x_c$, with the Taylor coefficients of that side's $\alpha$
entering through multiplication matrices on the coefficient vectors, and
impose \eqref{eq:continuity} on $L^k u$ for $k = 0, 1, 2$ and on the flux
for $k = 0, 1$. These five conditions give a square \emph{continuity
matrix} per side, $C^- a^- = C^+ a^+$ on the coefficient vectors $a^\pm$.
Keeping the monomials on the evaluation node's side and carrying them
across with $(C^+)^{-1} C^-$ gives the \emph{translated basis}: piecewise
polynomials, one per monomial, that satisfy the five conditions. The weights
of a five-node stencil that straddles $x_c$ reproduce $L$ at the evaluation
node on this basis, a $5 \times 5$ solve per stencil, and a stencil that
straddles both edges of a thin layer translates twice.

\para{Two dimensions}
A stencil whose $30$ nodes lie in more than one region is given a local
frame at the point of each interface nearest its centre, $x'$ along the
tangent and $y'$ along the normal, in units of the stencil's radius, and
the interface is expanded there as $y' = f(x')$. The continuity conditions
equate, along the interface, the first $5 - 2k$ coefficients in $x'$ of
$L^k u$ and the first $4 - 2k$ of the normal flux
$\alpha (\partial_{y'} - f' \partial_{x'}) L^k u$, fifteen conditions for
degree four, one per bivariate monomial; the curvature enters through $f$,
in the curve along which the coefficients are read and in the normal
derivative. The translated basis replaces the monomials $p_j$ in
\eqref{eq:rbffd}. The Gaussians are \emph{warped}: in the frame of the
evaluation node's side, the normal coordinate of every node is replaced by
a piecewise-linear one whose slope changes by the factor $\alpha^- /
\alpha^+$ across each interface, so that a Gaussian's value and its
$\alpha\, \partial_n$ are both continuous there and the RBF part of the
stencil satisfies the flux condition to first order.

This construction, with warped Gaussians, is what we call
\emph{jump-aware}. Applied with the centre of a smooth edge as the
interface and the pieces' Taylor data as if $\delta$ were $0$, it is the
\emph{$\delta = 0$ construction} of Sections~\ref{sec:construction1d}
and~\ref{sec:naive2d}. It is also the $\delta \to 0$ limit of the seeds,
which reproduce it at $\delta = 0$ (Sections~\ref{sec:jumplimit}
and~\ref{sec:assembly2d}).

Fix a stencil of the grid of Section~\ref{sec:setting1d}: five nodes
$x_1 < \cdots < x_5$ and an evaluation point $x_e$, one of them. The
standard weights are exact on the monomials $(x - x_e)^k$, $k \le 4$, and

\subsection{Seeds as the profiles of solutions polynomial in time}
\label{sec:chain}
% TRACE: stiff 1.2 (the time-polynomial solutions, (j + 1) g_{j+1} = L g_j;
% one power of L per power of t against the wave equation's two; the heat
% polynomials of Rosenbloom-Widder; the definition; the seeds one at a
% time; the five continuity conditions in order, one field). Lemma
% lem:heatpoly's general u_k is E5.5's, added to stiff 1.2 with this
% ticket (the chain's algebra, no number).
Look for solutions of $u_t = L u$, $L = \px \alpha \px$, that are
polynomial in time, $u = \sum_{j = 0}^{J} t^j g_j(x)$. Matching powers of
$t$ gives $(j + 1)\, g_{j+1} = L g_j$ and $L g_J = 0$, so the profile at
$t = 0$ satisfies $L^{J+1} g_0 = 0$. The heat equation spends one power of
$L$ on each power of $t$ where the wave equation spends two, but the
profiles fill the same nested kernels of $L$. With a constant $\alpha$
they are the polynomials: the time-polynomial solutions of
$u_t = \alpha u_{xx}$ are the heat polynomials
\begin{equation*}
  v_k(x, t) = \sum_{0 \le j \le k/2} \frac{k!}{j!\, (k - 2j)!}\,
  x^{k - 2j}\, (\alpha t)^j
\end{equation*}
\cite{RosenbloomWidder1959,Widder1975}, whose profiles at $t = 0$ are the
monomials. The seeds are the same profiles for a variable $\alpha$,
anchored at the evaluation point.

\begin{definition}[Seeds]
\label{def:seeds}
Let $\alpha > 0$ and $\alpha_e = \alpha(x_e)$. The \emph{seeds} at $x_e$
are the functions $\phi_0, \phi_1, \ldots$ of the chain \eqref{eq:chain}:
$\phi_0 = 1$, $\alpha \phi_1' = \alpha_e$ and
$L \phi_k = k (k - 1)\, \alpha_e\, \phi_{k-2}$ for $k \ge 2$, with
$\phi_k(x_e) = 0$ for $k \ge 1$ and $\phi_k'(x_e) = 0$ for $k \ge 2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:heatpoly}
For every $k$ the function
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:heatpoly}
  u_k(x, t) = \sum_{0 \le j \le k/2} \frac{k!}{j!\, (k - 2j)!}\,
  (\alpha_e t)^j\, \phi_{k - 2j}(x)
\end{equation}
solves $u_t = L u$, and $u_k(\cdot, 0) = \phi_k$. With a constant
$\alpha$, $\phi_k = (x - x_e)^k$ and $u_k(x, t) = v_k(x - x_e, t)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $c_j = k! / (j!\, (k - 2j)!)$. By the chain,
$L u_k = \sum_j c_j\, (k - 2j)(k - 2j - 1)\, \alpha_e^{j+1}\, t^j\,
\phi_{k-2j-2}$ and $\pt u_k = \sum_j (j + 1)\, c_{j+1}\, \alpha_e^{j+1}\,
t^j\, \phi_{k-2j-2}$, and the two agree because
$(j + 1)\, c_{j+1} = c_j\, (k - 2j)(k - 2j - 1)$. With $\alpha$ constant,
$L (x - x_e)^k = \alpha\, k (k - 1) (x - x_e)^{k-2}$ and the anchor
conditions fix the two constants of integration, so $\phi_k = (x - x_e)^k$
by induction; with $\alpha_e = \alpha$, \eqref{eq:heatpoly} is then
$v_k(x - x_e, t)$.
\end{proof}

Read one at a time, the seeds say what each monomial becomes through the
edge. The seed $\phi_1$ solves $L \phi_1 = 0$: it is the equilibrium of
constant flux, $\phi_1(x) = \alpha_e \int_{x_e}^{x} d\xi / \alpha(\xi)$,
and what stays smooth through the edge is its flux $\alpha \phi_1'$, not
its slope. The seed $\phi_2$ is the profile of $u_2 = \phi_2 + 2 \alpha_e
t$, whose rate of change is constant, $u_t = 2 \alpha_e$; the constant
matters, since $u_t = 0$ would only return $\ker L$. In general, by
\eqref{eq:heatpoly}, $\pt^m u_{2m} = (2m)!\, \alpha_e^m$ is constant and
$\pt^m u_{2m+1} = (2m + 1)!\, \alpha_e^m\, \phi_1$ has a constant flux:
the even seeds carry $\pt^m u = \text{const}$ and the odd ones
$\alpha\, \px \pt^m u = \text{const}$. Taken in the order
$\phi_0, \ldots, \phi_4$ these are the five conditions of
\eqref{eq:continuity} from which the translated basis of a jump is built,
on $u$, its flux, $u_t = L u$, the flux of $u_t$, and $u_{tt} = L^2 u$. In
the wave equation the same alternation passes between two fields; in
diffusion both kinds of condition bear on $u$, so one chain serves every
stencil.

% TRACE: stiff 1.5's closing note and LITERATURE.md 1a K2 (the identity
% with q = 0, u_0 = r = 1, p = alpha, x_0 = x_e; the recursive integrals
% without a factor n, X^(k) for odd k and X~^(k) for even k, their weights
% 1/alpha and 1 alternating; the lambda-series coefficients the
% time-polynomial profiles). Checked in
% tests/heat1d/test_stiff.py::test_the_seeds_are_the_formal_powers_of_the_
% sturm_liouville_operator at 1e-9 relative through the two-constant
% medium's delta = 0.01 edge (the E5.2 pass found 12 digits for k <= 5).
% eq:nestedint is that identity with the recursion unrolled.
In one dimension the seeds are a classical object. Integrating the chain
twice from the anchor gives
$\phi_k(x) = k (k - 1)\, \alpha_e \int_{x_e}^{x} \alpha(s)^{-1}
\int_{x_e}^{s} \phi_{k-2}(r)\, dr\, ds$, and unrolling the recursion,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nestedint}
  \phi_k = k!\, \alpha_e^{\lceil k/2 \rceil}\, I_k, \qquad
  I_k(x) = \int_{x_e}^{x} \frac{ds_1}{\alpha(s_1)} \int_{x_e}^{s_1} ds_2
  \int_{x_e}^{s_2} \frac{ds_3}{\alpha(s_3)} \cdots,
\end{equation}
where $I_0 = 1$ and $I_k$ has $k$ nested integrals whose weights alternate
between $1/\alpha$ and $1$, starting from $1/\alpha$ outermost. These are
the recursive integrals, $X^{(k)}$ for odd $k$ and $\tilde X^{(k)}$ for even
$k$, by which Kravchenko and Porter write the solutions of $(p u')' + q u =
\lambda r u$ as power series in the spectral parameter
\cite{Kravchenko2008,KravchenkoPorter2010}, here with $q = 0$, $r = 1$,
$p = \alpha$, the particular solution $u_0 = 1$ and the integrals taken
from the anchor. The seeds are thus the formal powers of the
Sturm--Liouville operator $L$ anchored at the evaluation point, and the
coefficients of those series are the time-polynomial profiles, with
$\lambda$ in the role of $\pt$. The repository's tests check the identity
against the ODE march below to a relative $10^{-9}$ through a $\tanh$ edge.

% TRACE: stiff 1.2 (the moment conditions, the same ones E1.2 imposes on
% the translated basis); 2.3 P4 (constant alpha = 0.7: 0.7 times

% LITERATURE.md 6a's HODIE and fitted-operator sentence, verbatim (#45's

% (Samarskii-Makarov, Gavrilyuk et al.: the seed stencil "a variant of

The weights $w_i$ of a stencil approximate $L u$ at $x_e$ from the nodal
values by reproducing $L$ on the first five seeds,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:moments}
  \sum_{i=1}^{5} w_i\, \phi_k(x_i) = (L \phi_k)(x_e) =
  \begin{cases} 2 \alpha_e, & k = 2, \\ 0, & k = 0, 1, 3, 4, \end{cases}
\end{equation}
since $L \phi_k = k (k - 1)\, \alpha_e\, \phi_{k-2}$ vanishes at $x_e$ for
every $k$ but $2$. These are the conditions the interface-aware stencils
impose on the translated basis, and with a constant $\alpha$ they return
$\alpha$ times the standard second-derivative weights \cite{Fornberg1988},
which the implementation reproduces to rounding. Stencils exact on a local
space other than the polynomials go back to fitted operators and to HODIE
\cite{AllenSouthwell1955,Ilin1969,LynchRice1980,RoosStynesTobiska2008}.
Three-point schemes whose coefficients come from Cauchy problems
integrated across a piecewise-smooth coefficient are the exact and
truncated schemes \cite{SamarskiiMakarov1990,GavrilyukEtAl2011}, and the
seed stencils are a five-point variant of them.

\para{First-order form}
% TRACE: stiff 1.3 (the state (phi_k, psi_k), psi_k = alpha phi_k'; the
% initial data; the march outward from x_e; a jump a segment boundary
% across which (phi, psi) is continuous, the weak reading; the
% double-cross), 2.3 (on each segment alpha is read from the piece of the
% medium's element that owns it, so delta = 0 is marched like any other
% width rather than dispatched to E1.2's algebra).
The chain is integrated as a first-order system in each seed and its flux
$\psi_k = \alpha \phi_k'$,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:firstorder}
  \phi_k' = \psi_k / \alpha, \qquad
  \psi_k' = k (k - 1)\, \alpha_e\, \phi_{k-2},
\end{equation}
with $\phi_0(x_e) = 1$, $\psi_1(x_e) = \alpha_e$ and every other initial
value zero, so that $\alpha$ is never differentiated. The ten states march
together from $x_e$ outward to each side of the stencil. Across a jump
$\phi$ and $\psi$ are continuous, which is $L$ read in the weak sense: the
march stops at the jump and reads $\alpha$ on each side from the piece
that owns it, so a jump is marched like any other width, and a stencil that
straddles both edges of a thin layer marches through both.

\para{Stencil coordinate}
% TRACE: stiff 1.3 (the chain invariant in xi; w = w~ / h_s^2), 2.3 (h_s =
% max |x_i - x_e|, xi_i = 0, +-1/2, +-1 on an interior row) and P5: 23.5
% the constant-alpha Vandermonde value, and 24 again for delta >= 10 h
% (tests, stiff 2.3); 90 at delta = 0 and 138 at delta = h/2 on the stencil
% of fig:seeds1d, paper/data/heat1d_stiff.json weights_vs_jump/matlab
% (paper_numbers.py, where "§3.1; stiff §2.3 P5").
In the coordinate $\xi = (x - x_e) / h_s$, with $h_s = \max_i |x_i - x_e|$,
the functions $\phi_k(x) / h_s^k$ satisfy the same chain with
$\alpha(x_e + h_s \xi)$ in place of $\alpha$. They are close to $\xi^k$, and
the matrix $A_{ki} = \phi_k(x_i) / h_s^k$ of \eqref{eq:moments} is a
perturbed Vandermonde matrix; the weights are $w = \tilde w / h_s^2$, with
$A \tilde w$ equal to the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:moments}. For the
centred stencil, $\xi_i = 0, \pm \tfrac12, \pm 1$, the condition number of
$A$ is $23.5$ with a constant $\alpha$. On the stencil of
Figure~\ref{fig:seeds1d}, half a cell from a $9 : 1$ edge, it is $90$ at
$\delta = 0$ and $138$ at $\delta = h/2$, and $24$ again once
$\delta \ge 10 h$, where the seeds are close to the monomials.

\para{The march and the seeded rows}
% TRACE: stiff 2.3 (DOP853 at SEED_RTOL 1e-13 / SEED_ATOL 1e-15; EDGE_STOP
% at the centre and +-10 delta; the rows whose window span meets (x_c - 20
% delta, x_c + 20 delta), TANH_REACH; 8 and 84 rows at delta = 0.0025 and
% 0.04 on 100 nodes; the batched march, some sixteen solve_ivp calls per
% operator, 0.11 s for the 1284 rows of delta = 0.04 at 1600 nodes: counts
% and timings from the notes, not a results file); 2.1 (the pieces to the
% bit beyond 20 delta); no threshold in delta, stiff 2.3's closing
% paragraph (the h <~ 2 delta regime costs the seeds nothing; at delta = 0
% they are E1.2's operator). E5.5 withdrew stiff 1.4's "an edge below
% about 1e-5 h is better served by the jump weights" (their solution is
% O(delta) from the smooth one, stiff 2.2), so it is not carried here. The
% march's floor: h^2 max |L_h u| over the seeded rows at 200 nodes,
% paper/data/heat1d_stiff.json row_residuals/matlab (paper_numbers.py,
% where "§3.1; stiff §2.3 P6"); the solution's 1.0e-11 at 101 nodes and
% delta = 5e-4 and 4e-11 at 100 and 1e-4, stiff 2.3 P6 (scratch, notes
% only); collocation not built, stiff 2.5's limitations.
The march is an explicit adaptive Runge--Kutta method of order eight
(SciPy's DOP853) at relative tolerance $10^{-13}$ and absolute tolerance
$10^{-15}$, with segment boundaries at each edge centre and at $10 \delta$
on either side of it, so that the step never has to find the edge inside a
long step over flat material. A row is seeded when the span of its stencil
meets $(x_c - 20 \delta, x_c + 20 \delta)$ for an edge centre $x_c$,
beyond which the edge is its pieces to the last bit; every other row is the
standard one. At $\delta = 0$ the seeded rows are exactly those that
straddle the jump; at $\delta = 0.0025$ and $0.04$ on $100$ nodes they
number $8$ and $84$; once $20 \delta$ covers the domain, every row is
seeded. No threshold in $\delta$ switches the seeds on or off: a resolved
edge costs them nothing (Section~\ref{sec:seedrates1d}), and at
$\delta = 0$ they are the jump's weights (Section~\ref{sec:jumplimit}).
Rows whose nodes sit at the same offsets from $x_e$ march as one system,
so an operator costs some sixteen calls of the integrator whatever the
number of seeded rows: $0.11$\,s for the $1284$ seeded rows of
$\delta = 0.04$ on $1600$ nodes. The march has one floor of its own. With
$L_h$ the discrete operator, the seeded rows' residual on the exact
equilibrium, $h^2 \max |L_h u|$, is at most $\sci{2.5}{-16}$ at $200$
nodes while the march crosses the edge in a few steps ($\delta \ge h/10$),
and $\sci{2.4}{-13}$ and $\sci{5.8}{-13}$ at $\delta = h/100$ and $h/1000$,
where it takes many. The equilibrium solution shows it once
$\delta \lesssim h/40$, at $10^{-11}$ to $\sci{4}{-11}$. A collocation
integration of the same chain would remove the floor; we have not built
one.

\subsection{Nested kernels and the jump as the limit}
\label{sec:jumplimit}
% TRACE: stiff 1.4 (the nested spaces; the spaces, not the basis,
% determine the weights; the weak reading; the translated x with slopes in
% the ratio alpha^+/alpha^-; for constant pieces the chain is the
% translated basis, one construction with two implementations); 2.3 P4 and
% 2.5 (the march against E1.2's algebra at delta = 0: 7e-16 in the
% weights, 3e-15 in the functions, paper/data/heat1d_stiff.json
% weights_vs_jump/matlab and seed_functions, paper_numbers.py where "§3.2;
% stiff §2.5 (2)"); LITERATURE.md 1a K3 (Sujecki, Gartland).
Since $L$ sends each seed two steps down the chain,
$\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0, \ldots, \phi_k\}$ is the $(k + 1)$-th space
of
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nested}
  \{1\} \subset \ker L \subset \ker \px L \subset \ker L^2 \subset
  \ker \px L^2 \subset \cdots,
\end{equation}
where $\ker \px L^m$ holds the $g$ with $L^m g$ constant. The spaces depend
on neither the anchor nor the normalisation, so the weights do not either;
only the basis does.

For a jump, read $L$ in the weak sense, $\phi$ and $\alpha \phi'$
continuous across it. Then $\ker L$ is spanned by $1$ and the
piecewise-linear function whose slope changes by the factor
$\alpha^- / \alpha^+$ across the jump, the translated $x$ of
Section~\ref{sec:jump}. On constant pieces $L \phi_2 = 2 \alpha_e$ makes
$\phi_2'' = 2 \alpha_e / \alpha^\pm$ on the two sides, with $\phi_2$ and its
flux continuous, which is the continuity of $u_t$ and of its flux; and so
on up the chain, each condition $\pt^m u = \text{const}$ or
$\alpha\, \px \pt^m u = \text{const}$ being one of \eqref{eq:continuity}.
For constant pieces the chain across a jump is therefore the translated
basis, the anchor conditions picking one basis of its span, and the
interface-aware stencils are the seed stencils at $\delta = 0$: one
construction with two implementations, algebra for a jump and an ODE march
for a smooth edge. On the stencil of Figure~\ref{fig:seeds1d} the march
reproduces the translated basis to $\sci{3}{-15}$ function by function, and
its weights to $\sci{7}{-16}$. The same device, Taylor data carried across
a step to make the weights, appears in photonics \cite{Sujecki2010}, and
three-point schemes of any prescribed order across a jump in the
coefficient are Gartland's \cite{Gartland1989}.

% TRACE: stiff 2.3 P4 (0.843, 0.465, 0.114, 0.0107, 0.00107 of the largest
% weight at delta/h = 1, 1/2, 0.1, 0.01, 0.001), paper/data/heat1d_stiff.json
% weights_vs_jump/matlab (paper_numbers.py, where "§3.2; stiff §2.3 P4");
% E5.5's number check: 0.465 is 46 %, not the 47 % of stiff 1.4's scratch;
% the functions first order too, stiff 2.5's table (5.12e-2, 5.12e-3,
% 5.12e-4 for phi_1 at delta/h = 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, tests); P8, the
% double-cross, stiff 2.3 (1 | 0.1 | 1 on [0, 2h] at h = 0.05: 7.6e-15 at
% delta = 0; 22.2 %, 1.86 %, 0.182 % at delta/h = 0.1, 0.01, 0.001; tests).
As $\delta \to 0$ the seed weights approach the jump's at first order in
$\delta / h$. On the stencil of Figure~\ref{fig:seeds1d},
$\max_i |w_i - w_i^{\mathrm{jump}}| / \max_i |w_i^{\mathrm{jump}}|$ is
$84$, $46$, $11$, $1.1$ and $0.11$ percent at $\delta / h = 1$,
$\tfrac12$, $0.1$, $0.01$ and $0.001$, and the seeds approach the
translated basis at the same rate. A stencil that straddles both edges of a
layer two cells thick, $\alpha = 1 \,|\, 0.1 \,|\, 1$, marches through

and $0.001$ they differ from them by $22$, $1.9$ and $0.18$ percent.

When the pieces vary the limit is not exact. The interface-aware
construction expands each side's $\alpha$ to degree four about the
interface and truncates the operator there (Section~\ref{sec:jump}), while
the seed $\phi_2$ solves $(\alpha \phi_2')' = 2 \alpha_e$ exactly,
$\phi_2 = (x - x_e)^2 - \tfrac23\, (\alpha'(x_e) / \alpha_e)\, (x - x_e)^3
+ \cdots$, so that within a stencil the two five-dimensional spaces differ
at relative order $h \alpha' / \alpha$. On the sinusoidal medium, whose
layer has $\alpha' / \alpha = 25$ at its edge, the seed and jump weights
of the row whose evaluation point is that edge differ at $h = 0.01$ by
$1.25$ times the largest weight at $\delta = 0$ and by $1.26$ to $2.24$
times for $\delta / h$ from $0.001$ to $1$, and the difference falls with
$h$.
Neither space is wrong, each being exact on its own five functions; on this
medium the seed operator's error is the smaller, by $6$ to $44$ times at
equilibrium and $6$ to $38$ times on the ramp problem at $\delta = 0$ over
$101$ to $1601$ nodes. There the seeds are measured against the reference
solution, never against the jump construction.

\subsection{Well-posedness}
\label{sec:ect}
% TRACE: stiff 1.5 (the proposition and its Polya form; the owner's
% alpha_e on a jump) with E5.5's note there: a complete Chebyshev system
% for piecewise-continuous alpha, an extended one where alpha is smooth
% (the classical theorem asks the weights for derivatives), proved from
% eq:nestedint, the seeds being multiples of the canonical nested
% integrals; LITERATURE.md 1a K7 (the companion's argument, on Polya,
% Karlin-Studden, Coppel).
The stencil solve is nonsingular for every edge width, the jump included,
and for every set of distinct nodes, not only the ones we used.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:ect}
Let $\alpha$ be positive and bounded away from zero on an interval $I$
containing $x_e$, and continuous on $I$ except at finitely many jumps,
across which $L$ is read in the weak sense. Then for every $k$ the seeds
$\phi_0, \ldots, \phi_k$ form a complete Chebyshev system on $I$:
interpolation in their span at any $k + 1$ distinct points of $I$ is
uniquely solvable. Where $\alpha$ is smooth, as through a $\tanh$ edge
between smooth pieces, they form an extended complete Chebyshev system. In
particular \eqref{eq:moments} has a unique solution for every
$\delta \ge 0$ and every five distinct nodes in $I$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:nestedint} the seeds are nonzero multiples of $I_0, \ldots,
I_k$, nested integrals whose weights $v_1 = 1/\alpha$, $v_2 = 1$,
$v_3 = 1/\alpha, \ldots$ (outermost first) are positive. Such a family is
the canonical basis of the kernel of the operator in P\'olya form
$\px \frac{1}{v_k} \px \cdots \frac{1}{v_2} \px \frac{1}{v_1} \px$, which is
$\px L^m$ for $k = 2m$ and $L^{m+1}$ for $k = 2m + 1$, and whose kernel
is the $(k + 1)$-th space of \eqref{eq:nested}. Suppose $g = \sum_{j \le k} c_j I_j$
vanishes at $k + 1$ distinct points of $I$. Then $g' = v_1 g_1$, where
$g_1 = \sum_{1 \le j \le k} c_j I'_{j-1}$ is a combination of the $k$
nested integrals $I'_0, \ldots, I'_{k-1}$ with the weights $v_2, v_3,
\ldots$, and $g_1$ is continuous. Between two consecutive zeros $a < b$ of
$g$, $\int_a^b v_1 g_1 = 0$ with $v_1 > 0$, so $g_1$ vanishes in $(a, b)$:
it has $k$ distinct zeros. By induction on $k$, $g_1 \equiv 0$, so
$c_1 = \cdots = c_k = 0$, and then $g = c_0$ vanishes, so $c_0 = 0$. Only
the positivity and integrability of the weights entered, so the argument
holds across finitely many jumps. Where $\alpha$ is smooth the operator is
disconjugate on $I$ and the family is an extended complete Chebyshev system
\cite{Polya1922,KarlinStudden1966,Coppel1971}.
\end{proof}

% node: alpha_e the owner's value, region_index putting a point on a jump
% to its right, PiecewiseAlpha.at_interface naming the owner; phi_1 slope
% 1 on the owner's side and alpha_e/alpha_other on the other); LITERATURE.md
% 1a K7 (the argument is the companion's).
When $x_e$ lies on a jump, as it does for the rows whose evaluation node
falls on an edge of the sinusoidal medium, $\alpha_e$ is the value of the
piece that owns $x_e$; $\phi_1$ then has slope $1$ on that side and
$\alpha_e / \alpha$ on the other, and nothing else changes, since
$\alpha_e$ enters the seeds only as a factor. The argument is the one the
companion paper makes for the two fields of the wave equation
\cite{Martin2026seeds}, shortened by \eqref{eq:nestedint}.

\subsection{Truncation error}
\label{sec:truncation}
% TRACE: stiff 1.6 (the Newton form with Muhlbach's remainder; M = d_x L^2,
% five derivatives; tau(g) = c_5 h^3 (M g) + ...; one order below the
% companion's first-derivative bound; E1.2's h^3 moments 0.32, 0.084 and
% 172 for the 1/9 | 1 jump at 1/2, 3/2 and 0 cells, zero to rounding for
% equal pieces, tests/heat1d/test_interface.py::test_jump_rows_have_an_h3_
% moment_and_equal_pieces_do_not); the global order four, stiff 1.6 (the
% bounded discrete Green's function; E1's rates 3.85-4.19 on the jump,
% port notes 1.3-1.4); LITERATURE.md 1a K7 (Beale-Layton's O(h)
% truncation at the interface, O(h^2) solution: cited here, not claimed).
Interpolation in an extended complete Chebyshev system has a Newton form
whose remainder carries the operator that annihilates the space where the
polynomial remainder carries the $(k + 1)$-th derivative
\cite{Muhlbach1973}. For the five seeds that operator is $M = \px L^2$, and
the local truncation error of a seed stencil on a smooth function $g$ is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:truncation}
  \tau(g) = \sum_{i=1}^{5} w_i\, g(x_i) - (L g)(x_e) = O(h^3\, M g),
\end{equation}
with a constant that the remainder expresses through $1/\alpha$ inside the
stencil. That is third order, not fourth: a second derivative on five
points is generically third order, and the centred standard stencil gains
its fourth only because its $h^3$ term vanishes by symmetry, which an edge
breaks. The interface-aware rows of a jump show the same. On the function
of degree five in their translated basis, which lies outside the space they
reproduce, the $h^3$ term is $0.32$, $0.084$ and $172$ (in the stencil
coordinate) for a $9 : 1$ jump half a cell and one and a half cells from
the evaluation node and at the node itself, and zero to rounding for equal
pieces.

The global order is still four. A local error of order $h^3$ on a fixed
number of rows costs one order less in the solution than the same error on
every row, since the inverse of the discrete operator has entries of
order $h$, so the solution error is $O(h^4)$. This is what the
interface-aware stencils reach at a jump (rates $3.85$ to $4.19$,
elliptic and parabolic, in our port of \cite{Martin2016}), and it is the
phenomenon Beale and Layton prove for interface corrections, where an
$O(h)$ truncation error at the interface leaves the solution second order
\cite{BealeLayton2006}. The bound \eqref{eq:truncation} has two readings,
and Section~\ref{sec:results1d} is built on them.

\para{Equilibrium}
% TRACE: stiff 1.6's first reading (u = A + B phi_1 in every seeded
% window; the seeded rows exact at any delta and h).
If $L u = 0$ then $M u = 0$, and more than that: $u$ has a constant flux,
so on every seeded stencil it lies in $\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,
\phi_1\}$, and \eqref{eq:moments} reproduces $L u$ there exactly, at any
$\delta$ and any $h$ (Section~\ref{sec:elliptic1d}).

\para{Time dependence}
% TRACE: stiff 1.6's second reading (M u = d_x u_tt, bounded independently
% of delta, changing by the contrast ratio across the edge; FD4's u^(5) ~
% delta^-4; the naive rows miss an O(1) fraction of the slope jump, O(1/h)
% in L u; the constant's delta-independence not a consequence of the
% proposition, P7); 2.3 (the separable solution e^{ct} v on the
% two-constant medium, 100 -> 400 nodes: the seeded rows' residual rates
% 3.06, 3.78 at delta = 0.01 and 3.15, 3.51 at 0.0025, residual n^3 0.27,
% 0.26, 0.15 and 0.43, 0.38, 0.27, within a factor two between the widths;
% the plain rows' 3.94, 3.97 and 3.94, 4.01; tests, notes only); the
% smoothing paragraph, stiff 1.6's last (the flux changes by int u_t dx =
% O(delta) across the edge; why the construction is a serious baseline).
If $u_t = L u$ then $L^2 u = u_{tt}$ and $M u = \px u_{tt}$, the
$x$-derivative of another solution of the heat equation. Since $u_{tt}$
has a continuous flux, $\px u_{tt}$ is bounded independently of $\delta$
and changes across the edge by the ratio of the pieces, whatever the width.
The standard stencil's error is driven by $u^{(5)}$ instead, which grows
like $\delta^{-4}$ inside the edge, and the rows of $D_x A D_x$ that see an
unresolved edge miss a fixed fraction of the jump in slope, an error of
order $1/h$ in $L u$. The bound does not make the constant in
\eqref{eq:truncation} independent of $\delta / h$, since the remainder
involves $1/\alpha$ across the stencil. Measured on a separable solution
$e^{ct} v(x)$ of the two-constant medium from $100$ to $400$ nodes, the
seeded rows' residual is third order (rates $3.06$ to $3.78$), its
constants at $\delta = 0.01$ and $0.0025$ within a factor of two of each
other, and the standard rows' residual is fourth order ($3.94$ to
$4.01$).

Diffusion smooths, so for $t > 0$ every $\pt^k u$ is itself a solution and
the second reading holds for the whole chain. And since
$\px (\alpha u_x) = u_t$, the flux changes across an edge of width $\delta$
only by $\int u_t\, dx = O(\delta)$: in diffusion a sub-grid edge is close
to a jump at all times. That is why the $\delta = 0$ construction is a
serious baseline here (Section~\ref{sec:construction1d}), and why the
seeds' margin over it is set by $\delta$.

\subsection{The elliptic problem in one dimension}
\label{sec:elliptic1d}
% TRACE: stiff 1.8 (the exact constructions; the face conductance a_{i+1/2}
% = h / int dxi / alpha, exact for any profile; the plan's T1 not item 3;
% the correction for E3.5 with E5.5's note there: no cell mean sampled at
% the nodes makes Dx A Dx exact, while a nodal alpha read off the discrete
% solution would; tests/heat1d/test_operators.py::test_no_nodal_alpha_
% makes_dx_a_dx_exact_on_a_kinked_equilibrium, a residual above 0.5 at 100
% nodes for one and two cells), 1.6's first reading, 2.5 (4) (the
% sinusoidal medium: the plain rows not exact, the conservative scheme
% exact); LITERATURE.md 1a K1 (Tikhonov-Samarskii, Samarskii; Patankar
% section 4.2-3, pp. 44-47, eqs. 4.9-4.10, derived from the steady,
% no-source, one-dimensional case; Pan-Xu-Li's harmonic average method,
% their eq. 3, second order their theorem; Goode-Appel's statement of
% exactness), K10 (i) (T1 the like-for-like changed-medium comparator,
% citing nothing); plan 3.2 and R2.
The one-dimensional equilibrium has a constant flux $\alpha u' = B$, so
$u = u(-1) + B \int_{-1}^{x} d\xi / \alpha$, and two constructions are
exact on it at every $h$ and every $\delta$ when the pieces are constant.

The first is the seed operator. Its seeded rows are exact by the first
reading of Section~\ref{sec:truncation}, and its standard rows see a
linear function on a constant piece. At $\delta = 0$ it is the
interface-aware operator of the jump.

The second is the conservative three-point scheme with the face
conductances
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:faceconductance}
  a_{i+1/2} = h \Big/ \int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} \frac{d\xi}{\alpha(\xi)},
\end{equation}
whose discrete flux $a_{i+1/2} (u_{i+1} - u_i) / h$ equals $B$ at the
exact nodal values whatever $\alpha$ is, so that the exact solution
satisfies every discrete equation. It is the homogeneous scheme of
Tikhonov and Samarskii \cite{TikhonovSamarskii1962,Samarskii2001}, whose
face coefficient is the conductance of the constant-flux seed over one
cell. For a piecewise-constant $\alpha$ it is the harmonic-mean interface
conductivity of finite-volume heat conduction, which Patankar derives from
the same steady one-dimensional flux \cite[\S4.2-3]{Patankar1980} and which
is the exact interblock transmissivity of groundwater models for steady
one-dimensional flow through blocks of uniform transmissivity
\cite{GoodeAppel1992}. Pan, Xu and Li call the scheme
the harmonic average method and prove it second order
\cite{PanXuLi2026}. The exactness belongs to the conservative form: the
same harmonic mean sampled at the nodes and used as $A$ in $D_x A D_x$,
the like-for-like changed medium of Section~\ref{sec:treatments1d}, leaves
a residual above $0.5$ on the exact equilibrium of the $9 : 1$ jump at
$100$ nodes, whether the mean is taken over one cell or two.

So on constant pieces the one-dimensional equilibrium cannot rank the seeds
against the conservative scheme. It is the sanity check and the limit
case, and the one-dimensional ranking is the parabolic problem's, where
the conservative scheme is second order and the seeds are fourth
(Section~\ref{sec:treatments1d}). Both exactness results are classical,
and so is the conservative scheme's second order; we observe them and claim
none of them. Where the pieces vary, as in the sinusoidal medium, the
equilibrium does rank the two, for a reason that has nothing to do with
the edge: the conservative scheme stays exact, while the seed operator's
standard rows inside the layer keep the error of a smooth coefficient, and
it is exact only where the seeded rows cover the layer
(Section~\ref{sec:seedrates1d}).

With $\delta > 0$ each medium blends its pieces across each edge by
\eqref{eq:edge}, the two edges of the sinusoidal medium in turn, so that the
pieces' weights remain a partition of unity; at $\delta = 0$ the blend is the
jump to the last bit. Both references cut $[-1, 1]$ into elements on which
$\alpha$ is smooth up to their ends: the pieces for a jump, and for an edge
also the points $x_c \pm m\delta$, $m = 1, 3, 9, 27$, so that outside the
core $|x - x_c| \le \delta$ each element spans at most a factor of three in
distance from the centre and is resolved by a polynomial of modest degree.

The equilibrium reference is $u = u(-1) + B \int_{-1}^{x} d\xi / \alpha$,
with $B$ fixed by $u(1)$ and the integral taken by Gauss quadrature on the
elements. Through a $\tanh$ edge between constants $a$ and $b$ the
integrand has the antiderivative
\begin{equation*}
  \delta \Bigl[ \frac{z}{a} + \frac{a - b}{2ab}\, \ln \bigl(a + b\, e^{2z}\bigr)
  \Bigr], \qquad z = \frac{x - x_c}{\delta},
\end{equation*}
which the quadrature reproduces to $10^{-14}$; subtracting the jump's
$z / a$ and $z / b$ on the two sides leaves the deficit \eqref{eq:deficit}
as $z \to \pm\infty$. The parabolic reference is Chebyshev collocation on
the same elements, $20$ points on each and elements wider than $0.1$ split,
with $u$ and $\alpha u_x$ matched at every element end, integrated to
$t = 2$ by SciPy's implicit Radau method at relative and absolute
tolerances $10^{-9}$ and $10^{-11}$; the absolute tolerance sits above the
round-off of the collocation system on elements of width of order $\delta$,
which a tighter tolerance would make the step control chase. Against a second
resolution, $24$ points and elements to $0.05$, it agrees to
$\sci{5}{-12}$ on the two-constant medium and $\sci{6}{-11}$ on the
sinusoidal one, at worst over the four widths. That is round-off, not
truncation, and the finest errors of the seeds sit on it
(Section~\ref{sec:seedrates1d}).

Against each $\delta > 0$ line the figures also draw the \emph{floor}
$\|u_0 - u_\delta\|_2 / \|u_\delta\|_2$ at the nodes, the distance between
the jump problem's solution $u_0$ and the smooth one $u_\delta$, from the two
references. It is what a method that converges to the jump's solution
reaches (Section~\ref{sec:construction1d}).

The $\delta = 0$ construction converges to the jump problem's solution, so
while it converges the floor bounds its error from below. It has two
regimes (Figure~\ref{fig:knee1d}, purple, and Table~\ref{tab:knee1d}).

\para{Unresolved}
While the grid does not resolve the edge, the construction sits on the
floor. At $\delta = 0.0025$ on the two-constant medium its equilibrium error
at $50$, $100$ and $200$ nodes is the floor to three digits, and wherever
$h \ge 2\delta$ on that medium it lies at $0.93$ to $1.00$ times the floor,
at equilibrium and on the ramp problem. The floor is first order in
$\delta$, $0.73$ to $0.75\,\delta$ at equilibrium and $0.29\,\delta$ on the
ramp problem at $t = 2$, since the edge's resistance falls short of the
jump's by $c\,\delta$ with $c = 8.79$ \eqref{eq:deficit}, which the two
quadratures reproduce to six digits at every width; the flux through the
medium changes by $c\,\delta / F_0 = 0.88\,\delta$ relative to the jump's,
$F_0 = 10$ being the jump's total resistance. On the sinusoidal medium the
floor is not yet proportional to $\delta$ at these widths: it is $0.76$,
$1.20$ and $1.44\,\delta$ at equilibrium for $\delta = 0.04$, $0.01$ and
$0.0025$, and the measured deficit is $0.44$, $0.74$ and $0.91$ of the
limit $20.7\,\delta$ of its two $10 : 1$ edges. The sinusoid has
$\alpha' / \alpha = 25$ where it meets the edges, so that at
$\delta = 0.01$ it changes by $75\%$ within $3\delta$ of the edge, and the
pieces the edge blends are not the constants that \eqref{eq:deficit}
assumes. There the coarse rows also carry the sinusoid's own error, and the
construction lies at $1.05$ to $2.3$ times the floor where $h \ge 2\delta$.

\para{Resolved}
Once the grid resolves the edge the construction leaves the floor and its
error grows. Its plain rows are then fourth-order rows on a smooth
coefficient, but its three or four rebuilt rows still impose the translated
basis, a kink of slope ratio $\alpha^+ / \alpha^-$ at the edge's centre, on
a solution whose slope hardly changes across the centre cell, and the
discrete solution takes the kink. On the two-constant medium the error then
follows $h / \delta$, not $\delta$: at $h \approx \delta/2$ it is $0.0685$
to $0.0687$ for all three widths, which is $2.4$, $9.2$ and $37$ times the
floor at $\delta = 0.04$, $0.01$ and $0.0025$. At $1600$ nodes it reaches
$0.11$ at equilibrium for $\delta = 0.01$, and for $\delta = 0.04$ it is
$0.125$ at equilibrium and $0.048$ on the ramp problem ($0.22$ for both on
the sinusoidal medium). Between the two regimes, at $h \approx 2\delta$, the
partly resolved plain rows pull the solution toward the smooth one, and the
error dips just below the floor, to $0.93$ of it at $\delta = 0.0025$.

So treating the edge as a jump is the right baseline exactly where the grid
cannot see the edge, and the worst of the three operators where it can.
Using it means knowing $\delta$, switching it off near $h = 2\delta$, and
accepting a floor that falls only as $\delta$. At $\delta = 0$ it is the
interface-aware operator of \cite{Martin2016}, exact at equilibrium on the
two-constant medium and fourth order on the sinusoidal one (rates $3.88$ to
$4.12$).

at $100$ nodes and a quarter from $200$, and it is where the first rate of
$6.4$ comes from.

\para{A resolved edge costs nothing}
Across the knee, where the naive operator changes order and the
construction leaves its floor, the seeds' line does not move: it is the same
fourth-order line at every $h / \delta$ from $16$ down to $1/8$, below
which the references' floor takes over, with no threshold in $\delta$
(Section~\ref{sec:chain}). At $200$ nodes and $\delta = 0.0025$, where
$h = 4\delta$, the seeds' error on the ramp problem is $\sci{1.6}{-9}$,
against the naive operator's $\sci{7.9}{-4}$ and the construction's
$\sci{7.2}{-4}$. The construction's floor falls as $\delta$, so the seeds'
margin over it is set by $\delta$ and closes at $\delta = 0$, where on
constant pieces the two are one operator.

\para{The sinusoidal medium}
At equilibrium the seeded rows are exact on the sinusoidal medium too, and
the seed operator is exact only where the seeded rows cover the layer. On
both problems the rows whose stencils lie farther than $20\delta$ from both
edges are standard rows on the sinusoid, and their error is the line. At
$\delta = 0.04$ the reach covers the layer, every row is seeded, and the
ramp problem's error is $\sci{2.6}{-8}$, $\sci{1.5}{-9}$ and
$\sci{1.1}{-10}$ at $101$, $201$ and $401$ nodes (rates $4.1$ and $3.8$),
and then the references' floor. At $\delta = 0.01$ the reach covers the
layer at $101$ nodes only; from $201$ on it leaves the rows near
$x = 0.25$ standard, which carry $\sci{1.4}{-8}$ at $201$ nodes, and the
line is fourth order past them. With every row seeded instead, the
$\delta = 0.01$ errors at $201$ nodes are $\sci{1.3}{-13}$ at equilibrium
and $\sci{1.7}{-9}$ on the ramp problem; the reach is the operator's one
parameter, and we left it at $20\delta$. At $\delta = 0.0025$ and at the
jump the interior of the layer is standard at every count, and the line is
the sinusoid's own error under the standard fourth-order rows, which is
still pre-asymptotic, the rows beside the edges seeing
$h \alpha' / \alpha = 0.5$ at $101$ nodes. At $\delta = 0$ it falls from
$\sci{1.3}{-4}$ to $\sci{1.4}{-8}$ over $101$ to $1601$ nodes at rates
rising from $2.7$ to $3.75$, and at $\delta = 0.0025$ its rates rise from
$2.7$ to $4.0$. At $\delta = 0$ that line is still $6$ to $38$ times below
the construction's (Section~\ref{sec:jumplimit}).

\para{Stability}
The seed operator's interior spectrum, with the Dirichlet rows removed as
BD4 steps it, is real and negative on both media at $\delta = 0$ and
$0.0025$, and its extreme eigenvalue is the standard stencil's
$-16/(3h^2)$ in the material with $\alpha = 1$, to $0.6\%$ at $50$ nodes.
On the sinusoidal medium at $49$ and $53$ nodes, where
$h\alpha'/\alpha \approx 1$ at the layer's edges, the seed operator is
stable, with largest eigenvalue $-2.05$, while the $\delta = 0$
construction has eigenvalues at $+248$ and $+8.8$; the sweep starts at
$101$ nodes so that all three operators share its grids.

\label{sec:treatments1d}

The standing alternative to a new stencil changes the coefficient and
keeps the scheme (Section~\ref{sec:priorwork}). We ran four such treatments
on the grids and problems above, against the same references:
\begin{description}
\item[T1] the harmonic mean $(b - a) / \int_a^b d\xi / \alpha$ of $\alpha$
  over a window $[a, b]$ of one or two cells centred on each node, clipped
  at the ends of the domain and sampled into $A$ in $D_x A D_x$;
\item[T2] the arithmetic mean over one cell, used the same way;
\item[T0] the edge widened to $\max(\delta, m h)$, $m = 1$ or $2$, a
  coefficient regularised on the scale of the grid
  \cite{TornbergEngquist2003};
\item[T1-FV] the conservative three-point scheme with the face
  conductances \eqref{eq:faceconductance}.
\end{description}
T1-FV is Pan, Xu and Li's harmonic average method \cite{PanXuLi2026}, exact
at the one-dimensional equilibrium (Section~\ref{sec:elliptic1d}). T1 is
the like-for-like changed medium: a harmonic cell mean, which as a face
conductance makes the conservative scheme exact at equilibrium, sampled at
the nodes of the fourth-order operator, where it does not; we found no
source that uses it there. The harmonic mean is the exact conductance
between blocks of uniform conductivity, and may be inferior to other means
where the conductivity varies smoothly between nodes
\cite{GoodeAppel1992}, as it does here once $\delta > 0$. A fifth
candidate, the band-limited coefficient of the seismic literature
\cite{Mittet2017,KoeneWittstenRobertsson2022}, was not found in use for
diffusion and is left out. The comparison is against coefficient sampling
on the same nodes and against our implementation of the cell harmonic and
arithmetic means, a widened edge and, in one dimension, the conservative
scheme with exact face conductances, on one contrast and one test problem
per dimension.

\subsection{A straight feature}
\label{sec:straight}
% TRACE: stiff 3.2 (the frame at the foot point with the origin moved to
% the evaluation node; xi, eta in stencil radii; alpha_n the profile along
% the normal; the ansatz over the levels of a's parity; the chain read in
% 2-D; the level ODE in the flux variable; the anchor data; triangular in
% degree and level; 22 levels, 44 states for degree 4; the monomials on a
% constant alpha by induction), 3.3 (the stencil coordinate, w = w~/h_s^2).
% The time-polynomial solution u_ab is E5.7's, added to stiff 3.2 with this
% ticket (the chain's algebra, no number), the 2-D form of lem:heatpoly.
\para{Frame and anchor}
A seeded stencil keeps the orientation of the jump-aware frame: at the foot
point of the interface nearest the evaluation node, $x'$ along the tangent
and $y'$ along the normal (Section~\ref{sec:jump}). Its origin moves to the
evaluation node $x_e$, which lies on the normal through that foot point,
and lengths are measured in the stencil's radius $h_s$, the largest
distance from $x_e$ to a node of the stencil:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:frame2d}
  \xi = (x' - x'_e) / h_s, \qquad \eta = (y' - y'_e) / h_s .
\end{equation}
The material along the normal line, $\alpha_n(\eta) = \alpha(x_e + h_s \eta
n)$ with $n$ the unit normal, is what the seeds read, and
$\alpha_e = \alpha_n(0)$. In these coordinates $L = h_s^{-2} (\partial_\xi
\alpha \partial_\xi + \partial_\eta \alpha \partial_\eta)$; as in
Section~\ref{sec:chain} we drop the factor $h_s^{-2}$ in what follows and
restore it in the weights, $w = \tilde w / h_s^2$.

\para{The ansatz}
Where $\alpha$ depends on the normal coordinate alone, as it does exactly
across the flat edges of case 1, the seed of the monomial $\xi^a \eta^b$ is
a polynomial in $\xi$ whose coefficients are functions of $\eta$,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ansatz}
  \phi_{ab}(\xi, \eta) = \sum_{j} g_j(\eta)\, \xi^j, \qquad
  j = a, a - 2, \ldots, a \bmod 2,
\end{equation}
because $L (g(\eta) \xi^j) = \xi^j (\alpha_n g')' + j (j - 1)\, \alpha_n g\,
\xi^{j-2}$: the operator lowers the degree in $\xi$ by two or not at all. The
chain \eqref{eq:chain} read in two dimensions is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:chain2d}
  \phi_{00} = 1, \qquad
  L \phi_{ab} = \alpha_e \bigl[ a (a - 1)\, \phi_{a-2, b} + b (b - 1)\,
  \phi_{a, b-2} \bigr],
\end{equation}
the constant-coefficient operator's action on the monomial with the lower
monomials' seeds on the right. Collecting $\xi^j$ and writing the flux
$\psi_j = \alpha_n g_j'$ gives a first-order pair for each seed and level,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:levels}
  \begin{aligned}
  g_j' &= \psi_j / \alpha_n, \\
  \psi_j' &= \alpha_e \bigl[ a (a - 1)\, g_j^{(a-2, b)} + b (b - 1)\,
  g_j^{(a, b-2)} \bigr] - (j + 2)(j + 1)\, \alpha_n\, g_{j+2}^{(a, b)},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where a superscript names the seed a level belongs to and a missing level is
zero. The initial data are the monomial's at its top level,
$g_a(0) = [b = 0]$ and $\psi_a(0) = \alpha_e [b = 1]$, and zero for every
other level; for $b \ge 2$ the source generates $\eta^b$, as
$\phi_k(x_e) = \phi_k'(x_e) = 0$ did in one dimension. Seed $(a, b)$ at level
$j$ needs the same level of the seeds $(a - 2, b)$ and $(a, b - 2)$ and level
$j + 2$ of itself, so the system is triangular in total degree and in level,
and the fifteen seeds of degree at most four are $22$ levels with $44$
states, marched together as one linear system that never differentiates
$\alpha$. With a constant $\alpha$ the seeds are the monomials, by induction
on the degree as in Lemma~\ref{lem:heatpoly}. The proof of that lemma,
applied once in each index, shows that
$u_{ab} = \sum_{i \le a/2,\, k \le b/2} c^{a}_{i}\, c^{b}_{k}\,
(\alpha_e t)^{i + k}\, \phi_{a - 2i, b - 2k}$, with $c^{a}_{i} = a! / (i!\, (a - 2i)!)$, solves the
heat equation in these coordinates with $u_{ab}(\cdot, 0) = \phi_{ab}$: the
seeds are again the profiles of solutions polynomial in time.

% TRACE: stiff 3.2 (the four closed forms; phi_20 = xi^2 + g_0 with
% (alpha_n g_0')' = 2 (alpha_e - alpha_n), the "u_t = const" condition for a
% temperature quadratic along the edge; O(1) in stencil units across an
% unresolved edge; what a 1-D construction cannot supply; sin 2 pi x v(y) not
% in the span of the 1-D seeds), fig:seeds2d.
\para{Closed forms}
The linear seeds are the one-dimensional ones along the normal, stretched
along the tangent: $\phi_{10} = \xi$, $\phi_{01} = \alpha_e \int_0^\eta
d\eta' / \alpha_n$, the constant-flux seed $\phi_1$ of
Section~\ref{sec:chain}, and $\phi_{11} = \xi \phi_{01}$; $\phi_{02}$ is the
seed $\phi_2$ along the normal. The first seed that is not a
one-dimensional seed times a power of $\xi$ is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:phi20}
  \phi_{20} = \xi^2 + g_0(\eta), \qquad
  (\alpha_n g_0')' = 2 (\alpha_e - \alpha_n), \quad g_0(0) = g_0'(0) = 0.
\end{equation}
$L \xi^2 = 2 \alpha_n$ is not constant across the edge, and $g_0$ is the
normal profile that restores $L \phi_{20} = 2 \alpha_e$, the constant rate
of change of a temperature quadratic along the edge. It vanishes on a
constant $\alpha$ and is of order one in stencil units across an
unresolved edge (Figure~\ref{fig:seeds2d}), and no one-dimensional
construction supplies it: the two-dimensional content of the seeds is this
coupling of tangential monomials to normal profiles. It is also why the
elliptic problem tests them in two dimensions where it could not in one
(Section~\ref{sec:elliptic1d}): case 1's equilibrium
$\sin 2\pi x\, v(y)$ is not in the span of the one-dimensional seeds at any
resolution.

\para{A symmetry, and the march}
The frozen problem is invariant under shifts along $\xi$, and matching
powers of the shift gives
$g_j^{(a, b)} = \binom{a}{j}\, g_0^{(a - j, b)}$, with $g_0^{(m, b)} = 0$ for
odd $m$: only nine level-zero functions are independent. The $44$-state
march reproduces the identity to $\sci{7}{-16}$ at every $\delta$ tested,
from the jump to $8h$, and the monomials on a constant $\alpha$ to
$\sci{1.4}{-15}$; we march all $44$ states because it is the coupled form
that carries over to a curved or tangentially varying edge
(Section~\ref{sec:curved}). The march is that of
Section~\ref{sec:chain}: SciPy's DOP853 at the same tolerances in the
stencil coordinate, from the anchor in both directions, restarted at every
node's $\eta$ so that node values are integrated and not interpolated, with
segment boundaries at each edge centre and at $10\delta$ on either side;
$\alpha$ on a segment is read from the piece that owns it, so a jump is
marched like any other width, and a band thinner than the stencil is crossed
in one march with both of its edges as boundaries. The true operator applied
to the marched seeds by twelfth-order differences on a $25 \times 801$ grid
leaves $L \phi_{ab}$ minus the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:chain2d} below
$10^{-10}$ relative, the floor of the differences.

\subsection{Stencil assembly}
\label{sec:assembly2d}
% TRACE: stiff 3.4 (EABE eq. 2 with the seed block; the right-hand side 2
% alpha_e on the quadratics plus h_s alpha_xi on xi, the frozen profile's
% omission; alpha_eta never appears; the jump-aware rows apply alpha lap +
% grad alpha . grad to all 15 functions), 4.3 ("the right-hand side is the
% true operator at the anchor", to 1e-11 by differences, tests).
\para{The saddle-point system}
The seeds take the monomials' place in \eqref{eq:rbffd}: $P_{ie} =
\phi_e(\xi_i, \eta_i)$, read off the march at the nodes, and the
right-hand side is the true operator on each seed at the anchor,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:moments2d}
  (L \phi_{ab})(x_e) = 2 \alpha_e\, [(a, b) \in \{(2, 0), (0, 2)\}] +
  h_s \alpha_\xi\, [(a, b) = (1, 0)],
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_\xi$ the tangential derivative of $\alpha$ at the anchor. The
chain gives the first term, since the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:chain2d} vanishes at the anchor for every seed but the two
quadratics, whose lower seed is $\phi_{00} = 1$. The second is what the
frozen normal profile leaves out: $\partial_\xi (\alpha \partial_\xi \phi) =
\alpha_\xi \phi_\xi + \alpha \phi_{\xi\xi}$, and at the anchor $\phi_\xi \ne
0$ only for $\phi_{10} = \xi$. The normal derivative of $\alpha$ never
appears, since the seeds satisfy $\partial_\eta \alpha_n \partial_\eta$
with the true profile. Where the jump-aware rows apply
$\alpha \nabla^2 + \nabla \alpha \cdot \nabla$ to all fifteen functions of
their basis, the seeds carry $\nabla \alpha \cdot \nabla$ through the march
and need it at the anchor once, in one entry.

\para{The warp is the constant-flux seed}
The jump-aware stencils warp the Gaussians' normal coordinate
piecewise-linearly, with its slope changed by the factor $\alpha^- /
\alpha^+$ at the interface (Section~\ref{sec:jump}). The seeds bring the
smooth version with them: the Gaussians are written in $(\xi, \tilde\eta)$
with
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:warp}
  \tilde\eta = \phi_{01}(\eta) = \alpha_e \int_0^\eta
  \frac{d\eta'}{\alpha_n(\eta')},
\end{equation}
read off the same march at every node. With $\tilde\eta' = \alpha_e /
\alpha_n$ the chain rule gives, at any point of the normal line,
\begin{equation*}
  L\, G(\xi, \tilde\eta(\eta)) = \alpha_\xi G_\xi + \alpha G_{\xi\xi} +
  (\alpha \tilde\eta')'\, G_{\tilde\eta} + \alpha \tilde\eta'^2\,
  G_{\tilde\eta\tilde\eta},
\end{equation*}
and $\alpha \tilde\eta' = \alpha_e$ is constant, the flux of $\phi_{01}$, so
the term in $G_{\tilde\eta}$, which the jump-aware rows carry as
$\alpha_\eta G_\eta$, cancels exactly. At the anchor
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gaussrhs}
  (L G)(x_e) = \alpha_e (G_{\xi\xi} + G_{\tilde\eta\tilde\eta}) +
  \alpha_\xi G_\xi ,
\end{equation}
the Gaussian's Laplacian at the warped offsets times $\alpha_e$, with the
seeds' tangential correction. At $\delta = 0$ on constant pieces
\eqref{eq:warp} is the jump-aware warp, to $\sci{1.8}{-15}$ at the nodes of
case 1's $576$ crossing stencils at $2500$ nodes. The implementation
assembles the general chain-rule form and checks the cancellation along the
whole marched line rather than assuming it, and takes $\varepsilon$ from
the physical offsets, as the jump-aware stencils do. Written in the frame's
own $(\xi, \eta)$ the Gaussians are plain and carry $\alpha_\eta G_\eta$;
Section~\ref{sec:flat2d} measures what the warp is worth.

\para{The jump as the limit}
At $\delta = 0$ on constant pieces across a flat interface, the seed of
$\xi^a \eta^b$ is the monomial on the anchor's side and, on the far side, a
polynomial of degree at most four, since the chain has constant
coefficients and polynomial sources there. It is continuous with its flux
at every $\xi$, and by induction down the chain so are $L^k \phi$ and their
fluxes: the fifteen conditions of the continuity matrices, all exact for
constant pieces on a flat line (Section~\ref{sec:jump}). The matrices being
square and nonsingular, the far side is the translated polynomial, and the
seeds and the translated basis are two bases of one space, anchored at the
node and at the foot point. With the same Gaussians the seed rows are
therefore the jump-aware rows, the two-dimensional form of the one
construction with two implementations of Section~\ref{sec:jumplimit}. Over
the $576$ crossing stencils of case 1 at $2500$ nodes the sine of the
largest principal angle between the two spans is $\sci{4.9}{-14}$, and the
weights differ by $\sci{1.6}{-12}$ relative with plain Gaussians and by
$\sci{7.6}{-12}$ with warped ones; assembled, the two operators agree to
$\sci{5.3}{-13}$. On a band one spacing thick, $333$ crossing stencils of
which $240$ reach all three regions, where the jump-aware construction
translates twice with a change of frame between the interfaces and the
march crosses both in one pass, the span distance is $\sci{1.7}{-14}$ and
the weights differ by $\sci{6.0}{-13}$. At $\delta > 0$ the seed span leaves
the jump's at first order in $\delta / h$, $0.46$ to $0.74\,\delta / h$
across four anchors, with no floor down to $\delta = 10^{-5} h$. On a curved
interface, or with pieces that vary along it, the two constructions differ
at $O(h)$, and Section~\ref{sec:results2d} measures each against the
reference.

\para{Which rows are seeded}
A row is seeded when the signed distances of its $30$ nodes to an edge's
centre curve span a range that meets $[-20\delta, 20\delta]$, beyond which
the edge is its pieces to the last bit (Section~\ref{sec:problem}), or when
its nodes straddle the curve; at $\delta = 0$ the rule is the jump-aware
construction's crossing test. The stencil groups follow the same rule one
size up: a node whose $42$-node stencil sees an edge joins the interface
group and gets a $30$-node stencil of degree four, so that no bulk stencil
sees an unresolved edge, and of the group the members whose own $30$ nodes
see it are seeded. A stencil of the boundary zone that sees an edge is
seeded on its own one-sided nodes, since the chain is anchored at the node
and marched only where nodes are. Once $20\delta$ covers the strip every
row is seeded. No threshold in $\delta$ switches the seeds off: a resolved
edge costs them nothing but the marches (Section~\ref{sec:flat2d}).

The seed stencils are the interface group's, $30$ nodes and degree four.
Our companion paper found degree-four seed stencils unstable across a sharp
edge in the wave equation and used $19$ nodes and degree three
\cite{Martin2026seeds}; the cause there was the footprint of the
hyperviscosity row the wave operator needs, which has no counterpart in an
implicit march of a dissipative operator, and the spectra of
Section~\ref{sec:spectra2d} show no eigenvalue in the right half-plane.
Column-scaled, the seed block's condition number stays within $0.46$ to
$2.5$ times the monomial block's on the same nodes at every $\delta$ from
$10^{-5} h$ to $8h$; unscaled, it carries the contrast, from $55$ with the
edge resolved to $212$ with it unresolved on the stencil below the band. A
seed row costs $1.2$ to $5$ times a jump-aware row, a few milliseconds.

\subsection{Curved and tangentially varying features}
\label{sec:curved}
% TRACE: stiff 3.5 (route (a): the straight-feature seeds along the true
% normal through the foot point, curvature dropped; the companion's route
% (a) on the same sine pair) and its E4.7 correction (moving a tanh edge by
% kappa x'^2/2 changes the values beyond it by that times the jump in the
% flux-carrying slope, whatever delta is: O(kappa h^2) in value, an O(kappa)
% truncation error in a row whose weights are O(h^-2); at delta = 0 route (a)
% is E2.3's curvature=False construction, EABE Fig. 10's first-order line; a
% tangential variation of alpha changes the flux ratio from node to node, a
% kinked correction outside the span, also O(1)); 4.6 (the two split and
% measured; H9), whose numbers section 6.5 quotes.
\para{A frozen profile}
For a curved edge the natural first step, the companion paper's for the
wave equation \cite{Martin2026seeds}, marches the straight-feature seeds
along the true normal through the foot point and drops the curvature. The
seeds' kink then lies on the tangent line instead of on the curve: a node
at tangential offset $x'$ is at normal distance $y' - \kappa x'^2 / 2 +
\cdots$ from a curve of curvature $\kappa$, while its seed value assumes
$y'$. Moving an unresolved edge by $\Delta$ changes the values beyond it by
$\Delta$ times the jump in the slope that carries the flux, whatever
$\delta$ is: an error of $O(\kappa h^2)$ in value and, in a row whose weights
are $O(h^{-2})$, a truncation error of $O(\kappa)$ that does not converge.
At $\delta = 0$ on constant pieces this frozen profile is the jump-aware
construction without its curvature terms, whose flat line in \cite[Fig.~10]{MartinFornberg2017heat}
is first order. A variation of $\alpha$ along an unresolved edge does the
same another way: the ratio of fluxes the seeds impose across it changes
from node to node, a kinked correction outside their span. Either term alone
makes a frozen profile inconsistent at $O(1)$ through an unresolved edge;
Section~\ref{sec:curved2d} measures the two apart.

% TRACE: stiff 3.10 (the foot curve's normal coordinates x = gamma(sigma) + d
% n(sigma), one-to-one inside the focal distance, FOOT_CURVATURE (stiff 4.6:
% kappa_max (|d_e| + 2 h_s) < 0.8); the diagonal metric m = |gamma'| (1 -
% kappa d); L in divergence form with no cross term; xi = mu_e (sigma -
% sigma_0)/h_s, eta = (d - d_e)/h_s, m^ = m/mu_e = 1 at the anchor; the
% normal line xi = 0 is route (a)'s; the foot curve a coordinate line).
% The notes' curve parameter sigma and scale factor m are theta and mu here:
% sigma is the blend weight and m the rows per unit length in section 2.
\para{The curve's own coordinates}
Write every point near the foot curve $\gamma(\theta)$, in its own
parameter, as $x = \gamma(\theta) + d\, n(\theta)$, with $n$ the unit normal
and $\kappa$ the signed curvature. These coordinates are one-to-one inside
the focal distance, and the implementation refuses a stencil whose normal
line reaches within a margin of it, $\kappa_{\max} (|d_e| + 2 h_s) \ge 0.8$,
with $\kappa_{\max}$ the curve's largest curvature and $d_e$ the anchor's
distance to it. The metric is diagonal, with scale factors
$\mu = |\gamma'| (1 - \kappa d)$ along $\theta$ and $1$ along $d$, so that
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:curvilinear}
  L u = \frac{1}{\mu} \Bigl[ \partial_\theta \Bigl( \frac{\alpha}{\mu}\,
  \partial_\theta u \Bigr) + \partial_d (\alpha \mu\, \partial_d u) \Bigr],
\end{equation}
in divergence form with no cross term. About the anchor $(\theta_0, d_e)$
the stencil coordinates are $\xi = \mu_e (\theta - \theta_0) / h_s$ and
$\eta = (d - d_e) / h_s$, with $\mu_e = \mu(\theta_0, d_e)$; the operator keeps
its form with $\hat\mu = \mu / \mu_e$ in place of $\mu$, and $\hat\mu = 1$ at the
anchor, so that $\xi$ is arclength along the anchor's parallel curve in
stencil radii. The normal line $\xi = 0$ is the frozen profile's, with the
same $\alpha_n$, segment boundaries and pieces. What changes is that the
foot curve is the line $\eta = -d_e / h_s$ at every $\xi$, so the edge is a
coordinate line and its conditions hold along one.

% TRACE: stiff 3.10 (the ansatz with every level to xi^4; the series
% alpha/m^ = sum A_k xi^k, alpha m^ = sum B_k xi^k, m^ = sum M_k xi^k, k <=
% 4; the chain kept verbatim, m^ L phi = m^ alpha_e S phi; the level flux
% psi_i = sum B_k g'_{i-k}; the pair; triangular in the first line; g_i and

\para{Coupled levels}
Keep the ansatz \eqref{eq:ansatz}, now with every level,
$\phi_{ab} = \sum_{j=0}^{4} g_j(\eta)\, \xi^j$, and expand the coefficients
of \eqref{eq:curvilinear} along the coordinate lines,
$\alpha / \hat\mu = \sum_k A_k(\eta)\, \xi^k$,
$\alpha \hat\mu = \sum_k B_k(\eta)\, \xi^k$ and
$\hat\mu = \sum_k M_k(\eta)\, \xi^k$, $k \le 4$. The chain
\eqref{eq:chain2d} is kept as it stands, multiplied through by $\hat\mu$.
With $S \phi_{ab}$ its right-hand side and $\psi_i = \sum_k B_k\, g_{i-k}'$
the coefficient of $\xi^i$ in the normal flux, collecting $\xi^i$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:coupled}
  \begin{aligned}
  g_i' &= \frac{1}{B_0} \Bigl[ \psi_i - \sum_{k \ge 1} B_k\, g_{i-k}'
  \Bigr], \\
  \psi_i' &= \alpha_e \sum_k M_k\, (S g)_{i-k} - \sum_k A_k\, (i + 2 -
  k)(i + 1)\, g_{i+2-k},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
a missing level being zero. The first equation is triangular, so $\alpha$
is still never differentiated, and $g_i$ and $\psi_i$ are continuous
through a jump on the coordinate line, level by level, so the march reads
its pieces one-sidedly as before. On a flat line with $\alpha$ a function
of $\eta$ alone, \eqref{eq:coupled} is \eqref{eq:levels} and the levels of
the other parity stay zero. The initial data are those of
Section~\ref{sec:straight}; the parity trim and the shift identity do not
survive.

\para{Every seed to level four}
The series coefficients are small: $A_k$ and $B_k$ are $O(h^k)$ and $M_k$
is $O(h^{k+1})$ for $k \ge 1$. Level $j$ of seed $(a, b)$ is therefore
$O(h^{j - a})$ above its top and contributes $O(h^{b + j})$ to a smooth
solution, and an order count would keep it only while $b + j \le 4$, $55$
levels. The count hides a constant. Across a jump the far side of the seed
of $\xi^a \eta^b$ carries the flux ratio $\alpha_e / \alpha_{\mathrm{far}}$
to the power $\lceil b/2 \rceil$, and the dropped levels multiply it by the
variation of $\alpha$ along the edge. On case 2 at $\delta = 0$ and $40{,}000$
nodes the cut chain's error was $\sci{3.55}{-8}$; with every seed to level
four it is $\sci{7.18}{-9}$, and two more levels on every seed change
nothing to two digits. The chain has $75$ levels, $150$ states.

\para{Right-hand sides, series and scope}
At the anchor only level zero of $L \phi$ survives, and its equation is
marched with every term, so the chain holds on the whole line $\xi = 0$ and
$(L \phi_{ab})(x_e)$ is $2 \alpha_e$ on the two quadratics and zero
otherwise. The entry $h_s \alpha_\xi$ of \eqref{eq:moments2d} is gone: the

satisfies $L \phi_{10} = 0$, so the march carries the tangential derivative.

\subsection{A thin resistive layer}
\label{sec:ring}
% TRACE: stiff 3.6 (two edges in one march: the ring thinner than h at
% every count, no node inside, one march through both circles along the
% radial normal), 3.11 (each change a switch that only a band with a gap
% turns on; cases 1 and 2 the same to the bit, checked on flat and
% tangential rows, both warps, delta = 0 and 0.0025); the resistance
% 1.5/(1 + sin sin/2) at every s, port notes 2.9 and section 2.3.
The ring of case 3 is $1/s$ wide and thinner than the node spacing at every
count (Section~\ref{sec:setting2d}). No node lies inside it, and a stencil
across its midline reaches both sides; its march crosses both circles in
one pass along the radial normal, with both edges as segment boundaries.
What the ring needs beyond the band is switched on only for a band whose
two circles carry their gap, so that cases 1 and 2 are unchanged to the last
bit.

% TRACE: stiff 3.6 (widths not radii: Circle(0.35 - 1/s) stores the width to
% 8e-8 at s >= 1e10, port notes 2.9) and its E4.8 note, 3.11 ("the width":
% two O(1) stops w/h_s apart, ulp(1)/(w/h_s), 1e-7 in the median and 6e-7 at
% worst over stencils at s = 1e11, notes only; Band.gap; the march in the

% "the ring's series": 1/(alpha m^) = 1.5 s across the ring at delta = 0;
% the Fornberg series' rounding past DOP853's absolute tolerance; a
% piecewise Chebyshev interpolant in eta per segment, RING_POINTS, checked
% to 1e-13 of the largest coefficient).
\para{The width and the series}
At $s \ge 10^{10}$ the stored radii $0.35 - 1/s$ and $0.35$ give the width
only to $\sci{8}{-8}$ relative, and a march between two segment boundaries
$w / h_s$ apart, both of order one in stencil units, crosses the width only
to $\mathrm{ulp}(1) / (w / h_s)$, about $10^{-7}$ at $s = 10^{11}$. The
band therefore carries the gap $1/s$ exactly, and the march runs in the
offset from the outer circle's crossing, so that the segment it crosses is
the gap to rounding. Across the ring $1 / (\alpha \hat\mu)$ is $1.5 s$ at
$\delta = 0$, and it multiplies the rounding of the sampled series into the
small tangential levels, past the integrator's absolute tolerance; on a
ring each segment's series are a piecewise Chebyshev interpolant in $\eta$,
checked against the samples to $10^{-13}$ of their largest coefficient, so
that the integrator's stages see a smooth function.

% TRACE: stiff 3.6's smooth ring and the decision on #39 (Brad, 2026-09-23),
% 3.11 ("the smooth ring"): the fold keeps 0.41 of the contact resistance at
% delta = w/10 and 0.01 at w/4 (s = 1e3), the difference of the edges in
% alpha about w (stiff 4.8, notes only); 1/alpha blended by s(d1/delta) -
% s(d2/delta), SmoothBand(composition="resistance"); the excess resistance
% the jump's w (1/alpha_in - 1/alpha_out) at every (s, delta); as s -> inf at
% fixed delta a layer of peak resistivity 0.75/delta (R/(2 delta), R = 1.5,
% the share's peak slope 1/2 times w/delta). Section 2.1 and 2.3 promise this
% definition. The sentence that follows is LITERATURE.md 6a's resistive-layer
% sentence, verbatim, moved here by #45's third cut (with its K9 citations).
\para{The smooth ring}
With $\delta$ comparable to the width or larger, the band's composition
\eqref{eq:edge} never reaches the ring's plateau and keeps only $0.41$ of
its contact resistance at $\delta = w/10$ and $0.01$ at $w/4$ ($s = 10^3$);
blending $\alpha$ by the difference of the two edges keeps a resistance of
about $w$, against the jump's $1.5$. Both lose the resistance that the jump ring holds fixed as $s$ grows. The smooth
ring blends the resistivity instead,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:smoothring}
  \frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{1}{\alpha_{\mathrm{out}}} +
  \Bigl( \frac{1}{\alpha_{\mathrm{in}}} - \frac{1}{\alpha_{\mathrm{out}}}
  \Bigr) \bigl[ \sigma(d_1 / \delta) - \sigma(d_2 / \delta) \bigr],
\end{equation}
with $d_1$ and $d_2$ the signed distances to the inner and outer circles, so
that its excess resistance $\int (1/\alpha - 1/\alpha_{\mathrm{out}})\, dn$
is the jump's, $w (1/\alpha_{\mathrm{in}} - 1/\alpha_{\mathrm{out}})$, at
every $(s, \delta)$. As $s \to \infty$ at fixed $\delta$ it tends to a
layer of peak resistivity $R / (2\delta)$, with $R$ the contact resistance,
as the jump ring tends to the resistance itself. A resistive layer thinner
than the grid is an imperfect interface in the limit
\cite{SanchezPalencia1974,PerrusselPoignard2013,Hashin2001}, and
strong-form schemes across such a contact need its jump conditions one
derivative higher in two dimensions \cite{LombardPiraux2003,LombardPiraux2006};
the flux seeds of Section~\ref{sec:ring} are that observation for the
diffusion operator.

% TRACE: stiff 3.11 ("the flux seeds": the rows beside the ring stall with
% the 15 seeds (4.8, the flux-seedless line, which section 6.6 quotes); a

% the near side; all five make it O(h^5); why E2.3's basis fits at O(h^5)
% not established, its Cartesian frame the likely source). The second

% flux-seed sentence, verbatim, with K9's citations inserted (6's preamble).
\para{The flux seeds}
With the fifteen seeds the rows beside the ring stall at fine counts
(Section~\ref{sec:ring2d}). Across a kink the flux enters the far side's
values multiplied by the distance beyond it; across a thin resistive layer
it enters with no such factor, as the jump $[u] = R F$ with $F$ the flux
through the layer. The seeds of degree four that carry a flux, those with
$b \ge 1$, have a flux at the ring that is a polynomial of degree three in
the offsets; the true flux differs from it by $O(h^4)$, which $R$ turns
into an $O(h^4)$ error in the far side's values and an $O(h^2)$ error in a
row that reads many of them. Fitted by least squares to an exact mode
through the ring, the fifteen seeds are $O(h^5)$ on a stencil's near side
alone but $O(h^4)$ on the rows with $10$ to $12$ of their $30$ nodes across
it, where the jump-aware basis is $O(h^5)$. On a ring the block therefore adds the five seeds of degree
five that carry a flux, those of $\xi^4 \eta$, $\xi^3 \eta^2$,
$\xi^2 \eta^3$, $\xi \eta^4$ and $\eta^5$, from the chain of degree five:
twenty seeds on thirty nodes, and with them the fit is $O(h^5)$. Their moment
conditions vanish at the anchor, so the right-hand side is unchanged. An
imperfect contact costs strong-form schemes a derivative in the jump
conditions, as Lombard and Piraux observed for elastic waves
\cite{LombardPiraux2003,LombardPiraux2006}; the flux seeds add the
functions of one degree higher that carry the flux, and nothing else. Why
the jump-aware basis, whose continuity conditions also carry the flux only
to degree three along the curve, fits at $O(h^5)$ with fifteen functions we
have not established; its Cartesian frame couples the circle's curvature
into the flux of its degree-four monomials, which is the likely source.

% TRACE: stiff 3.11 ("the Gaussians on a ring": the warp phi_01's level 0,
% g_0(eta); its higher levels carry the ring's resistance along xi (case 3's
% piece varies 3 : 1) into a shear of about 4 per stencil radius; the floor
% 3.3e-6 at 80,000 nodes, 11 times E2.3's, against 2.3e-7 with level 0 alone
% (notes only); level 0's flux and derivative at the anchor the full seed's,
% so the right-hand side is unchanged; epsilon: alpha_e a twentieth of the
% pieces' or less inside the smooth ring, g_0 compresses by that factor, the
% seed system's condition number to 1e8, epsilon from the nodes' spacing in
% (xi, g_0)).
\para{The Gaussians on a ring}
Two changes to the Gaussians, both measured on the ring. The warp is the
level zero of $\phi_{01}$ alone: its higher levels carry the ring's
resistance, which varies $3 : 1$ around it, along $\xi$ into the far side's
warped coordinate, a shear of about $4$ per stencil radius at every $h$, and
Gaussians on that coordinate put a floor under the ring's line,
$\sci{3.3}{-6}$ at $80{,}000$ nodes against $\sci{2.3}{-7}$ with level zero
alone. Level zero has the full seed's flux and derivative at the anchor, so
\eqref{eq:gaussrhs} is unchanged. And for an anchor inside a smooth ring,
where $\alpha_e$ is a twentieth of the pieces' or less, the warp compresses
the neighbours by that factor, so $\varepsilon$ is taken from the nodes'
spacing in the warped coordinates instead of the physical one.

% TRACE: stiff 3.11 ("the Gaussians on rows anchored in an edge", E4.12):

% 0.2-0.4 on the rows of 4.8's delta = 0.001 outlier; the probe keeps
% falling, stability fails; the anchor's share -w_e/sum|w_j| 0.08-0.13
% against plain's 0.25-0.27; plain Gaussians' wrong-sign anchor weights at
% delta = 0.0025; PIECE_TOL = 0.05, alpha_piece the delta = 0 band's alpha;
% both blocks on one march, the larger share kept (seeds.diagonal_share,
% Appendix B's eq. 93 with its sign; seeds.gaussian_choice); at delta = 0
% the warp bit for bit (all 4.10, scratch one-offs, notes only). The

\para{Rows anchored in an edge}
The warp's slope $\alpha_e / \alpha$ is $1$ across the anchor's side of the
stencil only where the anchor sits on its piece. The resistivity tail of a
smooth ring reaches several $\delta$ out, and an anchor in it has $\alpha_e$
below its piece's value, so the warp squeezes the anchor's own side, by
$0.2$ to $0.4$ on the rows concerned. Their consistency is not the problem,
since the truncation error on those rows keeps falling; their stability
is: the anchor's share of such a row, $-w_e / \sum_{j \ne e} |w_j|$, is
$0.08$ to $0.13$, against $0.25$ to $0.27$ with plain Gaussians. Plain
Gaussians fail the other way where the edge is nearly resolved: some of
those rows then get an anchor weight of the wrong sign. So on a ring a row whose
anchor is off its piece, $|\ln (\alpha_e / \alpha_{\mathrm{piece}})| > 0.05$
with $\alpha_{\mathrm{piece}}$ the jump's value there, is solved with both
Gaussian blocks on its one march and keeps the weights with the larger
diagonal share, the diagonal dominance of \cite[App.~B]{Martin2016} with
its sign; a row on its piece keeps the warp, and at $\delta = 0$ every
anchor is on its piece. Off the ring the rule helps on the flat band where
it fires and is mixed on case 2, where the row-by-row choice at near-ties
can do worse than either uniform choice, so it stays the ring's safeguard.

\para{The knee}
At the jump the naive operator is first order: its error falls from
$\sci{4.14}{-3}$ at $1250$ nodes to $\sci{2.07}{-4}$ at $160{,}000$, a fit of
$1.23$, with rates between successive counts from $-0.5$ to $3.1$ that belong
to the node sets. Every line carries that scatter: at one count and width the
naive error spreads by $1.03$ to $2.0$ times over three node sets, and on one
node set the rates alone do not show the knee. Divided by the jump's error on
the same nodes, the scatter cancels, and the knee is nearly a function of
$h/\delta$ alone. While $h \gtrsim 6\delta$ the naive error is $0.86$ to
$0.94$ of the jump's at equilibrium ($0.78$ to $0.97$ on the parabolic
problem); it falls to $0.19$ to $0.27$ at $h \approx 2\delta$, stalls at
$0.081$ to $0.084$ at $h \approx \delta$ for all three widths that reach it,
and drops to $0.006$ to $0.007$ at $h \approx 0.53\,\delta$ ($0.0026$ for
$\delta = 0.04$ at $0.52\,\delta$). Between $h \approx 2\delta$ and
$h \approx \delta/2$ the error falls $311$ and $211$ times at
$\delta = 0.01$ and $0.005$, while over the same counts the jump's falls
$8.1$ and $6.2$ times: a knee $38$ and $34$ times deep in the jump's units.

$\delta = 0.04$, resolved at every count, fits $5.31$ over $1250$ to
$160{,}000$ nodes, and the same operator with $\alpha \equiv 1$ on the same
nodes fits $4.98$ from $2500$. So the naive line follows the jump's only
while $h \gtrsim 6\delta$, and its knee spans
$4\delta \gtrsim h \gtrsim 0.75\,\delta$.

rows are equispaced in $x$, so a row's $\sin 2\pi x$ coefficient is
$e^{ct} v$ at that row for the separable mode; on each side of a curve the
flux $\alpha\, \partial_y u$ at the innermost row is read from the quadratic
through that side's three rows, without crossing the curve, and the
reference's same reading is subtracted. On the first rows off the edge the
naive operator's relative flux error is a function of $h/\delta$ alone to
within a factor of $1.7$, between runs at $\delta$ on $n$ nodes and at
$\delta/2$ on $4n$, which share $h/\delta$. While $h \ge 4\delta$ it is
$0.31$ to $0.86$ of the flux itself at every count, and at the jump it does
not converge: it stays at $0.34$ to $0.37$ from $10{,}000$ to $160{,}000$
nodes while the RMS error falls $20$ times. It is $3.5$ to $5\%$ at
$h \approx \delta$ and $1\%$ at $0.75\,\delta$
(Figure~\ref{fig:knee2d}, bottom left). The naive solution converges on
average, not at the edge.

\para{The parabolic problem}
The parabolic errors repeat the equilibrium's, at $0.67$ to $1.09$ times
them from $2500$ nodes on at every width, so the knee belongs to the
operator. At $1250$ nodes they do not: on that node set the naive operator
has an eigenvalue in the right half-plane at every width, at $+14.8$ to
$+25.1$, which BD4 at $\Delta t = h$ amplifies, and its parabolic error there
is $1.7$ to $2.0$ times the equilibrium's at the jump and for
$\delta \le 0.01$. The mode sits on the free nodes in the middle of the band,
and none of the naive operators has it at $2500$ nodes; every parabolic
naive number at $1250$ nodes carries it.

\para{The $\delta = 0$ construction}
The jump-aware operator applied at the edge's centre, with the pieces' data as
if $\delta$ were $0$, converges to the jump problem's solution. Its
\emph{floor} is the RMS at the nodes of the two references' difference,
$u_\delta - u_0$: $0.12$ to $0.25\,\delta$ over the four widths. While
$h \ge 2.1\,\delta$ the construction sits on the floor to three digits
(Table~\ref{tab:floor2d}); at $h \approx \delta$ it dips $5$ to $10\%$ below
it, as its rows that do not cross the edge, which read the smooth $\alpha$,
pull the solution part of the way to the smooth one; and then it grows, to
$5.0$ times the floor at $\delta = 0.01$ and $3$ times at $0.005$ on
$160{,}000$ nodes. At $\delta = 0.04$, resolved at every count, its error

$h \approx \delta$. The construction beats the naive operator only while
$h \gtrsim 3\delta$, and by at most $6$ times, so that using it means knowing
$\delta$ and switching it off near $h \approx 3\delta$.

\para{Spectra}
The interior spectrum of the seed operator at $1600$ nodes is that of the
warped jump-aware operator at every width (Table~\ref{tab:spectra2d} and
Figure~\ref{fig:spectra2d}): no eigenvalue in the right half-plane, the

the jump reproduces the port's warped jump-aware line to the digit: the
rightmost eigenvalue $-7.27$, $h^2 \min \mathrm{Re}\, \lambda = -13.19$,

growing mode at $1600$ nodes, $+1003$ at the jump, where BD4's root reaches
$1.038$. On case 2 the tangential chain, the frozen profile of
Section~\ref{sec:curved} and the jump-aware operator have no eigenvalue in
the right half-plane either, at any width. Our companion paper found
degree-four seed stencils unstable across a sharp edge in the wave equation
\cite{Martin2026seeds}; in diffusion, whose implicit march needs no
hyperviscosity, the spectra show no sign of it.

\para{The rule}

(Section~\ref{sec:assembly2d}), with no threshold in $\delta$. At
$40{,}000$ nodes that is $6\%$ of the rows at the jump, $25\%$ at

milliseconds a row, and at $40{,}000$ nodes and $\delta = 0.04$ about

\para{The frozen profile}
At the jump the frozen profile is first order on case 2, a fit of $1.33$ over

which the sweep holds fixed, and not its width. The global matrix's
conditioning was not measured.

\para{The smooth ring}

On scattered nodes there is no cell, and in place of the cell means of
Section~\ref{sec:treatments1d} we take discs: the harmonic mean of $\alpha$ over a disc of radius $h/2$ or $h$
about each node (T1), the natural scattered-node analogue of the
harmonic-mean conductance \cite{Patankar1980}, and the arithmetic mean over
the same discs (T2), each sampled into $A$ in the naive operator. Averaging the diffusivity
over a node cloud \cite{KrausEtAl2023} or over a pair of particles
\cite{ClearyMonaghan1999} is in use, and with pairwise means Kraus, Kuhnert
and Suchde observe first order at an interface
\cite{KrausKuhnertSuchde2025}; we found no source that averages over a disc
about each node. T0 widens the band's edges to $\max(\delta, mh)$, $m = 1$
or $2$. The disc integrals are exact to rounding, taken in the coordinates of
case 2's shear, in which both curves are coordinate lines. No conservative
scheme on scattered nodes, a twin of T1-FV with control volumes and face
conductances, was built, so that the two-dimensional comparators are the
naive operator and the $\delta = 0$ construction, and a statement about the
strongest low-order comparator is a statement about these six; as in one
dimension, the band-limited coefficient is left out.

The comparison is against coefficient sampling on the same nodes and against
our implementation of the cell harmonic and arithmetic means, a widened edge
and, in one dimension, the conservative scheme with exact face conductances,
on one contrast and one test problem per dimension. On cases 1 and 2
(Figures~\ref{fig:treatments2d} and~\ref{fig:treatments2dcurved},
Tables~\ref{tab:treatments2d} and~\ref{tab:treatmentsbest2d}) no treatment
improves on sampling by more than $1.75$ times in the RMS norm anywhere in
either sweep, the parabolic problem on case 1's $1250$ nodes aside, whose
growing mode every treatment carries (Section~\ref{sec:naive2d}). In the
maximum norm the coarsest node sets differ more, by up to $3.1$ times on
case 1 at $1250$ nodes and $2.4$ on case 2 at $2500$, where the sampled line
is itself an outlier; from $5000$ nodes on no treatment improves on sampling
by more than $1.60$ in the RMS norm or $1.55$ in the maximum norm. On case 1's
parabolic problem at the jump the seeds lie $2.3$ orders below the best of
the treatments at $1250$ nodes and $4.7$ orders below at $40{,}000$.

\para{Where they help and where they hurt}
A disc of half a spacing helps a little while the edge is two to three
spacings narrower than the grid, and hurts from there on: the half-spacing
means cross sampling at $h / \delta = 1.8$ to $3.5$, at every width and on
both cases and problems, as in one dimension they help only while
$h \gtrsim \delta$ (Section~\ref{sec:treatments1d}). At the jump the
half-spacing disc is sampling by construction on these node sets, whose
innermost rows lie exactly $h/2$ from the curve, so ``worse than sampling at
the jump'' is a statement about the radius-$h$ means and T0, not about
averaging $\alpha$ as such: from $5000$ nodes on the radius-$h$ harmonic mean
is $1.6$ to $3.4$ times worse than sampling there, the arithmetic one $1.3$
to $3.0$ times, and T0 $1.7$ to $8.2$ times. Once the grid resolves the edge
the disc means cap the naive operator at second order: at $\delta = 0.04$
they fit $1.95$ to $2.05$ and lie $584$ to $3100$ times above it at
$40{,}000$ nodes, the radius-$h$ line $4.00$ times the half-spacing one,
which is the $r^2/8$ term of a disc mean's expansion. T0 there is the naive

the flux error on the first rows off the edge is $0.30$ to $0.59$ under the
treatments that change $\alpha$ there, against sampling's $0.34$.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Section~\ref{sec:scope} lists what the implementation leaves out of the
2016--2017 methods, and Sections~\ref{sec:seeds1d} to~\ref{sec:results2d}
state each restriction of the experiments where it arises. This section
gathers them.

% TRACE: sec:scope (stiff 5.5, "what is not covered"; E2.10, #24, closed
% won't-do, plan D10); LITERATURE.md 1c's own exclusions (three dimensions;
% corners and triple junctions; anisotropic alpha; alpha depending on u;
% time-dependent media; edges meeting a Dirichlet boundary; profiles other
% than tanh); the tanh blend of alpha on the bands, of 1/alpha on the smooth
% ring (eq:edge, eq:smoothring); the degrees, stiff 3.4 and 5.5 (30 / 4
% seeded rows in a 42 / 5 bulk; FD4's five-point rows in 1-D), and the bulk
% passing the seeds on a resolved edge, stiff 5.3 (4) and sec:flat2d.
\para{Scope}
Every medium here is isotropic, independent of $u$ and of time, and at most
two-dimensional. The cornered interface of \cite[Sec.~5.4.5]{Martin2016}
and triple junctions are left to separate work, and no edge meets the
boundary of the domain: every two-dimensional edge is a curve periodic in
$x$ or a circle, clear of the Dirichlet boundaries. Every smooth edge is a
$\tanh$ blend of $\alpha$, except the smooth ring's, which blends
$1/\alpha$ (Section~\ref{sec:ring}). The seeds were run at one degree:
fourth-order stencils in one dimension, and in two, degree-four stencils of
$30$ nodes, to which the ring adds five seeds of degree five, inside a bulk
of degree five whose higher degree passes them on finer node sets once the
grid resolves an edge (Section~\ref{sec:flat2d}). Nothing here says what
the seeds do at another order.

% TRACE: sec:scope's contrasts (stiff 1.1, 3.1); the ring's contrast large
% only at a fixed resistance s w = 1, which the seeds carry, its per-stencil
% conditioning flat in s, stiff 5.3 (9) and 5.5, sec:ring2d; the far side's
% seeds carry the flux ratio to the power ceil(b/2), at most 2 at degree 4,
% sec:curved (stiff 3.10); the seed blocks' conditioning at 5 : 1, stiff 4.3
% H3, sec:assembly2d; the tail rows' instability, looked for off the ring at
% 5 : 1 alone, stiff 5.5 and 4.10, sec:ring.
\para{Contrast}
Each case has one contrast (Section~\ref{sec:scope}). The large contrasts
are the ring's, and they come with a fixed resistance, $s w = 1$, which the
seeds carry, so that their per-stencil systems condition independently of
$s$ (Section~\ref{sec:ring2d}). A large contrast across a single edge was
not run: there the seeds' values beyond the edge carry powers of the flux
ratio up to its square (Section~\ref{sec:curved}), and their blocks'
conditioning was measured at the contrasts above only
(Sections~\ref{sec:chain} and~\ref{sec:assembly2d}). The instability of
rows anchored in a layer's tail (Section~\ref{sec:ring}) was looked for off
the ring at $5 : 1$ alone.

% TRACE: sec:results2d's intro and sec:setting2d (one node set per count,
% from a fixed random seed; the naive error's spread over three node sets at
% one count and width, up to 2.0: paper_numbers "§6, §6.2, §7.1; stiff §4.2
% H10"); stiff 5.5 (every set keeps E2.1's straddling rows, unmoved by the
% seed; robustness to arbitrary node placement near an edge untested; a
% shifted-row layout, stiff 5.6's deferred runs); stiff 5.3 (4) ("no
% deterioration as delta -> 0 on these grids, not a proven delta-uniform
% constant: one node set per count, and each line holds delta fixed while h
% falls, so none holds h / delta fixed"); the norms, sec:problem and stiff
% 5.5; the smooth ring's far field, 74-96 % of the nodes, paper_numbers
% "§6.6, §7.1; stiff §5.3 (9)"; no ring line with a max-norm error, stiff
% 5.5 (the ring driver's max is max |u|).
\para{Node sets and norms}
Each node count has one node set, and at one count the naive operator's
error moves by up to a factor of $2.0$ between node sets
(Section~\ref{sec:naive2d}). Every node set keeps the six rows that
straddle each curve (Section~\ref{sec:setting2d}) and moves only the rest,
so the seeds were never run with nodes placed freely beside an edge, nor on
rows shifted against it. Each convergence line holds $\delta$ fixed while
$h$ falls, and none holds $h / \delta$ fixed, so that the widths' lines lie
within a small factor of one another shows no deterioration as
$\delta \to 0$ on these node sets and proves no $\delta$-uniform constant.
The two-dimensional errors are RMS errors over all nodes, except the smooth ring's, which are
taken over the $74$ to $96\%$ of the nodes away from it; the error near a
smooth ring, and any ring's error in the maximum norm, were not measured.

% TRACE: sec:seedrates1d (the ramp errors fully discrete at dt = h; BD4's
% share about half at 100 nodes and a quarter from 200: E4.10's roundtable
% scratch, stiff 5.6 and 2.5 (2), notes only); sec:results2d's intro (3-19
% BD4 steps, t = 0.1 over h at 1250 and 40,000, arithmetic; stiff 5.5);
% rem:elliptic1d and sec:elliptic1d (the 1-D equilibrium on constant pieces
% ranks nothing, plan 3.2); sec:seedrates1d and sec:treatments1d (the
% sinusoidal medium: the standard rows' pre-asymptotic line; T1-FV ahead at
% delta = 0 below 801 nodes, paper_numbers "abstract, §1, §4.5, §7.1, §7.3;
% stiff §2.5 (3)"); the reach of 20 delta the operator's one parameter,
% never varied, sec:seedrates1d and sec:flat2d (stiff 5.5).
\para{The test problems}
The seeds' one-dimensional parabolic errors are those of the fully discrete
scheme at $\Delta t = h$, of which BD4's share is about half at $100$ nodes
and a quarter from $200$ (Section~\ref{sec:seedrates1d}). The parabolic
problem of case 1 is $3$ to $19$ BD4 steps of one separable mode from its
analytic history, which tests the operators' spectra under BD4 more than a
transient through the edge. The one-dimensional equilibrium ranks nothing on constant
pieces (Remark~\ref{rem:elliptic1d}), and on the sinusoidal medium the
seeds' line at $\delta = 0$ and $0.0025$ is the pre-asymptotic error of the
standard rows inside the layer, which puts the conservative scheme ahead of
them at $\delta = 0$ below $801$ nodes (Section~\ref{sec:treatments1d}). The reach of
$20\delta$ that decides which rows are seeded is the operator's one
parameter, in either dimension, and it was never varied.

% TRACE: LITERATURE.md 1a K10 (iii) and 6b (our implementations, one
% contrast and one test problem per dimension); K10 (i) (the disc means: no
% source found, "the natural scattered-node analogue", sec:treatments2d);
% stiff 5.3 (10) and 5.5 (no conservative scattered-node scheme built); 1d
% and sec:treatments1d (T3, the band-limited coefficient, not found in use
% for diffusion and left out).
\para{The comparison}
The coefficient treatments are our implementations, each run on one contrast
and one test problem per dimension, and in two dimensions the cell means
are taken over discs about the nodes, a reading of the cell rule for
scattered nodes for which we found no source
(Section~\ref{sec:treatments2d}). No conservative scheme was built on the
scattered nodes, so the strongest comparator of the one-dimensional study,
the conservative scheme with exact face conductances, has no
two-dimensional counterpart here; the band-limited coefficient of the
seismic literature was left out in both dimensions
(Section~\ref{sec:treatments1d}).
The measurement licenses what Section~\ref{sec:priorwork} states and no
more; it is not a ranking of the methods in general.

% TRACE: sec:chain (the DOP853 floor at delta <~ h/40; collocation not built;
% stiff 2.3 P6); stiff 5.5 (the floor not seen in 2-D, and why the 2-D tests
% could not have seen it: 4.3's ladder measures distances to the jump's rows,
% its residual by twelfth-order differences floors at 4e-11); stiff 5.5
% (what the seeds need as input: alpha along each stencil's normal line at
% arbitrary points; the chain's foot points and curvature; the ring's gap;
% the naive product alpha at the nodes only); sec:curved (the guard
% kappa_max (|d_e| + 2 h_s) < 0.8; at delta = 0 the other curve only as a
% coordinate line of the foot curve's frame); stiff 5.5 (the guard refuses
% the tightest curves' coarsest sets: the 0.25 circle below 2500 nodes, some
% ring rows at 1250; two close non-concentric curved interfaces outside what
% was run); stiff 3.5 (route (b), a local boundary-value problem per stencil
% on a fine patch, named and not built); sec:curved2d (the chain 2.8-8.0
% times the flat seeds at the two coarsest counts: paper_numbers "§6.5,
% §7.1; stiff §5.3 (8)"); sec:flat2d (the warp loses once the edge is
% resolved; stiff 5.3 (7)); sec:ring (the rule: the stronger diagonal, a
% proxy, ring only; near-ties off the ring, stiff 5.2 and 5.5).
\para{The construction as built}
The march has a floor at $\delta \lesssim h/40$ in one dimension, which a
collocation integration of the chain would remove
(Section~\ref{sec:chain}). It was not seen in two dimensions, but the tests
there compare the seeds with the jump's rows and take residuals by finite
differences, and neither resolves a floor of that size. The seeds need a
continuous description of the medium, since the march samples $\alpha$ at
arbitrary points along each stencil's line; the tangential chain needs the
curve's foot points and curvature as well, and the ring its gap. The
standard stencils need $\alpha$ at the nodes only. The tangential chain
refuses a stencil whose line comes near the focal distance of its curve
(Section~\ref{sec:curved}), which excludes the coarsest node sets of the
tightest curves run, and at $\delta = 0$ it admits a second curve only as a
coordinate line of the first's frame, so that two close interfaces neither
parallel nor concentric are outside what was run. A local boundary-value
problem per stencil, in place of the march, was not built. On the two
coarsest node sets the tangential chain's error is $2.8$ to $8.0$ times the
flat seeds' (Section~\ref{sec:curved2d}). The warp
is kept for the unresolved regime and loses to plain Gaussians once the
grid resolves the edge (Section~\ref{sec:flat2d}). On the rows anchored in
a ring's layer, the choice between the two Gaussian blocks by the stronger
diagonal is a proxy for stability, and it is on for rings only
(Section~\ref{sec:ring}).

% TRACE: sec:spectra2d (H5 on case 1 only; the ring's iterative solves not
% run; stiff 5.3 (6)); sec:ring2d (the ring's per-stencil conditioning flat
% in s from 1e5 to 1e11, stiff 5.3 (9); the global matrix's conditioning not
% measured; the smooth ring's reference a 160,000-node seed run on the far
% field; one (s, delta) against a plain-built second fine run, the rule's
% fine solution 3.55e-6 from it: paper_numbers "§6.6, §7.1; stiff §5.3
% (9)"; the rule's convergence at s = 1e11, delta = 0.001 past 40,000
% unresolved); sec:ring (the jump-aware basis's O(h^5) fit with fifteen
% functions not established; twenty seeds with the flux seeds).
\para{The matrices and the ring}
Solvability was measured on case 1, where nothing breaks down as
$\delta \to 0$ (Section~\ref{sec:spectra2d}). On the ring the per-stencil
systems were measured at every $s$, but the global
matrix's conditioning was not, and its iterative solves were not run. The
smooth ring's reference is a fine run of the seeds themselves, read on the
far field, and one $(s, \delta)$ was read against a second fine run built
with plain Gaussians; there the rule's own fine solution is the discrepant
one, $\sci{3.55}{-6}$ from the other, so the rule's convergence at
$s = 10^{11}$ and $\delta = 0.001$ past $40{,}000$ nodes is unresolved
(Section~\ref{sec:ring2d}). Why the jump-aware basis fits the ring's mode at
$O(h^5)$ with fifteen functions, where the seeds need twenty, we have not
established (Section~\ref{sec:ring}).

% TRACE: timings, notes only. Stiff 4.5 and 5.5: a flat seeded row 1.3-4.4
% ms, which sec:assembly2d quotes as "a few milliseconds"; the 40,000-node

\para{Cost}
The marches are the construction's cost. A flat seeded row takes a few
milliseconds (Section~\ref{sec:assembly2d}), a row of the tangential chain
$15$ to $25$\,ms on an idle machine, and a ring row with the flux seeds
$40$ to $233$\,ms at $\delta > 0$ on a shared one; at $40{,}000$ nodes and
$\delta = 0.04$, with every row seeded, the marches take about $170$ seconds
against under three for the naive operator's assembly
(Section~\ref{sec:flat2d}). At a fixed $\delta > 0$ the seeded rows are
those within $20\delta$ of an edge, a fixed share of the nodes, so the
number of marches grows like the node count. These are the costs of a
reference implementation, and it is two-dimensional. In three dimensions
the seeds of a straight feature would be polynomials in two tangential
coordinates, $35$ of degree at most four against $15$, with coefficient
functions marched along the one normal, and those of a curved feature
would be marched in a surface's own coordinates; none of this was built,
and its cost is not known.

In the order we would do them.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The runs left open.} The drivers take these as they stand: a
  second fine run of the smooth ring at each of the five $(s, \delta)$ read
  against a single one, and the rule at $80{,}000$ and $320{,}000$ nodes at

\item \emph{A conservative scheme on the scattered nodes.} Control volumes
  about the nodes with the exact face conductances, the scattered-node
  form of the one-dimensional conservative scheme, are the comparator
  Section~\ref{sec:treatments2d} lacks. Pan, Xu and Li note that their
  harmonic average method is first order at a general interface in two and
  three dimensions, except where the interface is parallel to an axis
  \cite{PanXuLi2026}; built on scattered nodes, it would show how far the
  strongest low-order comparator gets through a sub-grid edge.
\item \emph{Seeded rows at the bulk's degree.} On a resolved edge the naive
  operator passes the seeds on finer node sets because its bulk rows have
  degree five and the seeded rows degree four
  (Section~\ref{sec:flat2d}). Seeded rows of degree five on the bulk's
  $42$ nodes, from the chain of degree five that the ring's flux seeds
  already march (Section~\ref{sec:ring}), should close that gap, which is
  untested, and are the first step to the orders above four that were not
  run.
\item \emph{A collocation integrator for the chain}, which would remove the
  march's floor at $\delta \lesssim h/40$ (Section~\ref{sec:chain}). The
  floor is $10^{-11}$ to $\sci{4}{-11}$ in the one-dimensional equilibrium,
  so it comes after the items above.
\item \emph{The geometry the tangential chain refuses.} A local
  boundary-value problem per stencil in place of the march along its
  lines, for the curves the guard of Section~\ref{sec:curved} refuses and
  for two close interfaces neither parallel nor concentric; and on the
  ring, the question Section~\ref{sec:ring} leaves open, why fifteen
  jump-aware functions fit its mode at $O(h^5)$, and a criterion other than
  the diagonal share for the Gaussians of the rows anchored in a layer.
\item \emph{Beyond the scope.} Edges that meet the boundary of the domain;
  the cornered interface and triple junctions; profiles other than
  $\tanh$, and large contrasts across a single edge; anisotropic,
  solution-dependent and time-dependent coefficients; and three
  dimensions, where a straight feature's seeds are polynomial in two
  tangential coordinates with their coefficient functions marched along the
  one normal, and a curved feature's are marched in a surface's own
  coordinates.
\end{enumerate}

Between a coefficient that varies on the scale of the grid and one that
jumps lies the smooth edge thinner than the node spacing. Through it the
standard stencils that sample the coefficient are first order until the
grid resolves the edge, and the interface-aware stencils of a jump, applied
at the edge's centre, sit on a floor of order $\delta$ and must be switched
off once the grid resolves the edge. The seeds are the profiles of
solutions polynomial in time, continued through the edge by ordinary
differential equations along the normal or along the edge's own
coordinates; in one dimension they are the classical formal powers of the
Sturm--Liouville operator, and as $\delta \to 0$ they reduce to the
interface-aware stencils of a jump. On scattered nodes the resulting RBF-FD
stencils are fourth order at every $\delta$ tested, elliptic and parabolic,
with no switch between regimes.

Through the flat band the seeds' elliptic fits at the four widths are
$4.23$ to $4.48$ over $1250$ to $40{,}000$ nodes, and the errors at the five
widths, the jump included, lie within factors of $1.2$ to $2.4$ of one
another at each count, which is no deterioration as $\delta \to 0$ on these
node sets, not a proven $\delta$-uniform constant; at $40{,}000$ nodes and $\delta = 0.0025$ their error is
$\sci{7.99}{-9}$ against the naive operator's $\sci{8.36}{-5}$. Through the
curved band the tangential chain is within $1.4$ to $3.9$ times of the flat
seeds' RMS error from $5000$ nodes at every $\delta$. On the ring thinned at
a fixed resistance, where the block adds five flux seeds, the seeds converge
at fourth order at every $s$, with fits of $4.43$ to $4.71$, at $0.52$ to $0.97$ times
the jump-aware operator's error from $5000$ to $40{,}000$ nodes, and
through a smooth ring whose width and edges are both below $h$ they
converge at fourth order on the nodes away from it. An imperfect contact
costs strong-form schemes a derivative in the jump conditions, as Lombard
and Piraux observed for elastic waves
\cite{LombardPiraux2003,LombardPiraux2006}; the flux seeds add the functions
of one degree higher that carry the flux, and nothing else. To our
knowledge no earlier scheme builds the basis of stencils on an unchanged
scattered node set through a smooth material edge thinner than the node
spacing by marching ordinary differential equations; its ingredients (the
exact face conductance, the formal powers, bases from local solutions of
the equation) are each classical, and in one dimension so is the
construction.

We compared the seeds with the coefficient treatments of diffusion
practice. The comparison is against coefficient sampling on the same nodes
and against our implementation of the cell harmonic and arithmetic means, a
widened edge and, in one dimension, the conservative scheme with exact face
conductances, on one contrast and one test problem per dimension. Through an
edge the grid does not resolve none of them is better than second order;
the conservative scheme is exact at the one-dimensional equilibrium and, on
the sinusoidal medium at $\delta = 0$, ahead of the seeds below 801 nodes,
where the seeds' line is pre-asymptotic; on the scattered nodes none
improves on sampling by more than a factor of 1.75 in the RMS norm, and the
seeds lie 2.3 to 4.7 orders below the best of them over 1250 to 40,000
nodes. What the seeds ask in return is a continuous description of the
medium and a march for each row within $20\delta$ of an edge; they need no
threshold on $h / \delta$.
